# Chemistry: how to solve

## General

- Use the atomic masses (or molar mass) given in the question. Molar mass is the sum of the atomic masses in the formula.
- Write down what you know and what you want, then pick the formula: `n = m / M`, `c = n / V` or `c1 × V1 = c2 × V2`.
- Volumes for concentration must be in dm³, so divide cm³ by 1000.
- Keep full precision in the calculator and round only at the end, to the precision the question asks for.

## Moles and molar mass

1. Work out the molar mass M: add the atomic masses, times each subscript.
2. Use `n = m / M`, or rearrange it to `m = n × M` or `M = m / n`.
3. For atoms of one element, multiply the moles of the compound by how many of those atoms are in its formula.
4. For particles, multiply moles by 6.02 (the answer is in units of 10²³).

Example: 36 g of H₂O, where M = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18, gives `n = 36 / 18 = 2` mol.

**Watch out:** a subscript after a bracket multiplies everything inside it. Ca(OH)₂ has 2 O and 2 H.

## Concentration and dilution

1. Use `c = n / V` with V in dm³. Rearrange it to `n = c × V` or `V = n / c`.
2. Convert cm³ to dm³ (÷ 1000) before using `c = n / V`.
3. If you start from a mass, find the moles first with `n = m / M`.
4. For dilution, use `c1 × V1 = c2 × V2`. Both volumes can stay in cm³. Water to add = final volume − starting volume.

Example: 0.5 mol in 250 cm³ gives `c = 0.5 / 0.25 = 2` mol/dm³.

**Watch out:** in a "how much water to add" question, the final volume from `c1 × V1 = c2 × V2` is not the answer yet. Subtract the volume you started with.

## Balancing equations

1. Pick an element that appears in the species with the missing coefficient.
2. Count that element's atoms on the other side (coefficient × subscript, added up).
3. Count its atoms already on the missing species' side, leaving out the missing term.
4. Solve `? × (atoms in one unit) + (atoms already there) = (atoms on the other side)`.

Example: in ? H₂ + 1 O₂ → 2 H₂O there are 4 H on the right, so `? × 2 = 4` and ? = 2.

**Watch out:** count atoms, not molecules, and remember the coefficient multiplies every atom in the formula.

## Percent composition and formulae

1. Percentage by mass: `(mass of the element in one formula unit ÷ M) × 100`.
2. Mass of an element in a sample: sample mass × (element mass ÷ M). To go the other way, divide by that fraction.
3. Empirical formula: divide each mass (or percentage) by its atomic mass, then divide every result by the smallest.
4. If a ratio ends in about .5, .33 or .25, multiply all of them by 2, 3 or 4.
5. Molecular formula: divide the molar mass by the empirical-formula mass, then multiply every subscript by that number.

Example: in H₂O, O is `16 / 18 × 100 ≈ 88.9` %.

**Watch out:** in the empirical formula, divide by the atomic mass, not by the percentage. And don't round 1.5 to 2: multiply everything by 2 instead.
