# Chemistry

Practise the core calculations of school chemistry. Every question tells you the atomic masses (or the molar mass) to use, so answers are never ambiguous. Mass is in grams (g), amount in moles (mol), volume in dm³ or cm³ and concentration in mol/dm³.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `n = m / M` | moles = mass ÷ molar mass |
| `M` | molar mass: add up the atomic masses in the formula, e.g. H₂O = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18 g/mol |
| `c = n / V` | concentration = moles ÷ volume in dm³ |
| `c1 × V1 = c2 × V2` | dilution: the moles of solute stay the same |
| `% = (mass of element ÷ M) × 100` | percentage by mass of an element in a compound |
| `N = n × 6.02 × 10²³` | number of particles (Avogadro constant) |

Useful conversions: 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 1 litre, so divide cm³ by 1000 to get dm³.

**Balancing:** one coefficient is replaced by **?** and every other coefficient is shown, including 1s. Count the atoms of one element on both sides to find it.

**Empirical formula:** divide each mass (or percentage) by its atomic mass, divide every result by the smallest, then multiply by a small whole number if needed (1.5 → × 2, 1.33 → × 3). The **molecular formula** is the empirical formula multiplied by (molar mass ÷ empirical-formula mass).

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Molar mass given, simple amounts, concentrations in dm³, short equations; exact answers |
| Medium | Molar mass from atomic masses, volumes in cm³, dilution, combustion equations, percentage by mass |
| Hard | Answers rounded as stated (often 2 or 3 decimal places), particles, solution preparation, larger equations, empirical and molecular formulae |

**Tip:** rounded answers are accepted within half a unit of the stated precision (for example ±0.0005 when rounding to 3 decimal places). Keep full precision in the calculator until the last step.
