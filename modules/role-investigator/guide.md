## General

These cases chain simple steps, so work in order and read every stated rule before you start.

1. Times are on the 24-hour clock. Add minutes by carrying over every full 60 into the hours.
2. Decide what a part asks for before calculating: a time, a count, a percentage or a verdict.
3. Round only as each part asks. The chained answers here do not change if you use an earlier rounded answer.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule. Watch words like "at or before", "more than" and "at least".

## Timelines and alibis

Arrival time = time last seen + journey time. A person could have been there if they arrive at or before the time of the event. When a task takes time after arriving, the latest arrival is the event time minus that task time.

To work backwards, take the task time off the event time, then take the whole journey (including any wait) off that to get the latest departure. A claimed departure after that is impossible.

## Statements and the liar

Try each person in turn as the one who took it, and mark which statements would then be false. Keep only the case where exactly one statement is false. That statement's speaker is the liar.

"It was not me" is true unless the speaker took it. "It was neither A nor B" is false if either one took it. To count possibilities after dropping the false statement, test each person against the remaining statements only.

## Records and patterns

A steady pattern rises by the same step each time. Find the step from entries that agree, then the odd one is the entry that does not fit. Percentage above = (recorded − expected) ÷ expected × 100.

For first digits, look at the leading digit only. Share = count ÷ total × 100. Expected count = stated share × batch size. A band of 25% means 25% of the expected count, above and below; "more than" means strictly more.

## Flags and base rates

Use counts, not just percentages. Fraudulent claims = true rate × all claims. Flagged and real = hit rate × fraudulent claims. Honest claims = all − fraudulent; wrongly flagged = false-alarm rate × honest claims.

The chance a flagged claim is real = flagged and real ÷ all flagged × 100. Working backwards, wrongly flagged = total flagged − flagged and real, and the false-alarm rate = wrongly flagged ÷ honest claims × 100.
