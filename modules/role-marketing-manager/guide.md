## General

Marketing scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (views, clicks, customers, months, $).

1. Read the stated rules first: what each stage counts, how LTV or break-even is found, the threshold in the rule, how returns diminish.
2. Write down which number each part asks for, and its rounding.
3. Whole people and whole units round **up** when you need enough, and stay whole when you count what happened.
4. For a verdict, find the numbers first, then compare them using the stated rule.

## Campaign funnel

Each stage is a percentage of the stage before: clicks = views × click-through rate, trials = clicks × trial rate, paying customers = trials × paying rate. Cost per customer = total cost ÷ paying customers.

Working backwards, divide instead: trials needed = goal ÷ paying rate, clicks needed = trials ÷ trial rate, views needed = clicks ÷ click-through rate. Round up at every stage and use the whole number in the next step. The cost of views = views ÷ 1,000 × the price per 1,000 views.

## Acquisition cost and payback

CAC = marketing spend ÷ new customers. Payback (months) = CAC ÷ monthly margin per customer.

LTV = monthly margin × months stayed, so LTV ÷ CAC says how many times a customer repays their cost. With monthly churn, the average stay is 1 ÷ churn (5% churn means 20 months).

Blended CAC = total spend ÷ total new customers across both channels. Do not average the two channel CACs. A channel's own CAC is its spend ÷ its customers.

## Return on ad spend and budget split

ROAS = revenue ÷ ad spend. Gross profit after ads = revenue × margin share − ad spend. Compare channels on ROAS, not on revenue.

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ margin share: at that ROAS the gross profit just pays for the ads. Above it the channel makes money.

When budget moves, the channel that loses money loses revenue at its own ROAS. The channel that gains earns its own ROAS on the first part of the moved money, and the lower stated rate on the rest. Change in gross profit = change in revenue × margin share, since total spend is unchanged.

## Promotion profit

Sale price = price × (100 − discount) ÷ 100. Profit per unit = price − cost. Today's profit = units × today's profit per unit.

Units needed to keep that profit = today's profit ÷ new profit per unit, rounded up. A forecast at or above that keeps the profit.

For two promotions, give each its own profit per unit and its own forecast units. Total profit = units × profit per unit. A free gift keeps the full price but its cost comes off every unit sold.
