# Finance Theory: how to solve

## General

- Use the calculator for the sums, and keep full precision until the end.
- With block answers, decide each column on its own.

## Sources of finance

1. **Internal:** from inside the firm (retained profit, selling off excess inventory). **External:** from outsiders (banks, suppliers, investors).
2. **Short term:** under a year, for day-to-day cash (overdraft, trade credit, debt factoring). **Long term:** over a year (loans, mortgages, debentures, shares, venture capital, retained profit).
3. Only a **share issue** or **venture capital** gives away part of the ownership.

**Hard.** Apply the stated order and stop at the first rule that fits: a need lasting weeks → overdraft; enough retained profit → retained profit; high gearing and owners open to new shareholders → share issue; otherwise → long-term bank loan.

## Risk and return

1. Higher expected return usually means higher risk. Safest to riskiest: savings account, government bonds, company bonds, large-company shares, start-up shares.
2. Expected return = Σ probability × return. With two equally likely outcomes it is their average.
3. Spread (risk) = good-year return − bad-year return. A cautious investor looks at the **worst case**.

Example: 60% chance of +10%, 40% chance of −5%: 0.6 × 10 + 0.4 × (−5) = 4%.

**Expert.** For a split, first find the portfolio's good-year and bad-year returns (weighted by the split), then weight those by the probabilities.

## Ratio interpretation

| Ratio | Formula | Better when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross profit margin | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | higher |
| Net profit margin | net profit ÷ revenue × 100 | higher |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities | higher (below about 2) |
| Acid test | (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities | higher (below about 2) |
| Gearing | non-current liabilities ÷ capital employed × 100 | lower |
| Receivables days | receivables ÷ credit sales × 365 | lower |
| Inventory days | inventory ÷ cost of sales × 365 | lower |
| ROCE | operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100 | higher |

**Hard.** Gross margin unchanged but net margin moved → overheads changed relative to sales. Both moved by the same points → cost of sales or selling prices changed.

## Investment appraisal

1. **Payback:** add the inflows until they cover the cost. Part year: months = amount still needed ÷ that year's inflow × 12. Quick and simple, but ignores cash after payback.
2. **ARR:** average yearly profit ÷ investment × 100. Uses the whole life, but ignores when returns arrive.
3. **NPV:** Σ inflow × discount factor − cost. Allows for the time value of money, but needs an estimated discount rate.
4. Which to use: money back fast (cash tight, fast-changing market) → payback; a target % return → ARR; value in today's money (long projects) → NPV.

**Expert.** When the methods disagree, NPV shows which project adds the most value for the owners.
