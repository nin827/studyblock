# Applied scenarios (Logistics Manager)

Scenario questions for the **Logistics Manager** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: a square-root formula, averages, percentages, rounding up to whole units, cases, pallets or doors, and comparing options. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Money is in a generic `$`. Every rule (the formula, the safety-stock rule, how duty is charged, how cartons sit on a pallet, how fast a door unloads) is stated in the question, and each part says how to round.

## Order quantity and inventory cost

How much to order each time so that ordering and holding costs balance. You use the economic order quantity (EOQ) formula, count the orders placed in a year and add up the yearly cost. Later you round the order up to whole cases and see what that costs, and at Expert+ you decide whether a supplier's quantity discount is worth the bigger orders.

## Safety stock and reorder points

A table of daily demand gives the average day and the busiest day. A stated rule turns the gap between them and the lead time into safety stock, and then into a reorder point that tells you when to order. At Expert+ a cheaper supplier is slower, so you set the extra safety stock it needs against the saving on price.

## Landed cost and suppliers

The price on the quote is not the cost of getting the goods to your warehouse. You add duty and freight to find the landed cost per unit, compare suppliers and find the saving on the order. At Expert+ duty is charged on goods plus freight, and a delay cost per day of lead time can change which supplier wins.

## Pallets, trailers and dock throughput

Cartons are stacked on a pallet in a fixed layout, so you count how many fit in a layer and on a pallet, then how many pallets an order needs and how many trailers carry them. At Expert+ the pallets arrive at a cross-dock, and you work out how long the doors take and whether a proposed number of doors is enough.
