## General

Logistics scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (units, cases, pallets, days, hours, $).

1. Read the stated rules first: the formula, how to round, what the duty is charged on, the pallet layout, the door speed.
2. Write down which number each part asks for, and its rounding.
3. Whole things (units, cases, pallets, trailers, doors) round **up** when you need enough, and **down** when you ask how many fit.
4. For a verdict, find the numbers first, then compare them using the stated rule.

## Order quantity and inventory cost

EOQ = √(2 × yearly demand × cost per order ÷ holding cost per unit). Orders per year = demand ÷ order size.

Yearly ordering cost = orders per year × cost per order. Yearly holding cost = half the order size × holding cost per unit (on average, half the order is in stock). At the EOQ the two are equal, so the yearly cost is twice either one.

When orders must be whole cases, divide the EOQ by the case size, round up, and multiply back. Cost the new size in the same way, and the extra cost is the difference from the cost at the exact EOQ.

With a quantity discount, holding cost is a percentage of the unit price, so a lower price lowers it too. Yearly total = purchases (demand × price) + ordering + holding. Work out one total for each choice and compare them.

## Safety stock and reorder points

Average daily demand = the days added up ÷ the number of days. Safety stock = (busiest day − average day) × (lead time + extra days), as the question states.

Demand during the lead time = average day × lead time. Reorder point = that + safety stock. Round up to a whole unit. Reorder when the stock on hand is at or below the reorder point.

For two suppliers only the lead time changes, so the extra safety stock = (busiest − average) × the difference in lead times. Extra holding cost = extra units × holding cost per unit per year. Yearly saving = price gap per unit × yearly demand (the stated number of days × the average day). The cheaper supplier is better when the saving beats the extra holding cost.

## Landed cost and suppliers

Goods value = units × unit price. Duty = duty rate × the value it is charged on (goods only, or goods plus freight as stated). Landed cost = goods + duty + freight. Landed cost per unit = landed cost ÷ units.

Compare suppliers on the landed cost per unit, not the unit price. The saving on an order = the gap per unit × the units.

A delay cost adds (cost per unit per day × lead time days) to each supplier's landed cost per unit. Compare once without it and once with it: the winner can change.

## Pallets, trailers and dock throughput

Along each side of the pallet: how many cartons fit = side ÷ carton side, rounded down. Cartons per layer = the two counts multiplied. Layers = height limit ÷ carton height, rounded down. Cartons per pallet = per layer × layers.

Pallets needed = cartons ÷ cartons per pallet, rounded up. Trailers = pallets ÷ pallets per trailer, rounded up. Space used = pallets ÷ (trailers × spaces per trailer) × 100.

At the dock: total pallets = trucks × pallets per truck. All doors together unload doors × rate pallets an hour, so hours = total ÷ that. To finish in the shift, one door unloads rate × shift hours, so doors needed = total ÷ that, rounded up.
