# Applied scenarios (Chef)

Scenario questions for the **Chef** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up kitchen scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: scaling a recipe, taking a yield percentage into account, rounding packs up, working out clock times backwards from service, costing a dish, percentages of a menu price and stock counts. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Money is in a generic `$`, weights are in grams and kilograms, and times are on the 24-hour clock. Every rule (the yield, pack sizes, plating time, hold limit, waste tolerance, target gross profit) is stated in the question, and each part says how to round.

## Scaling, yield and buying

Scale a recipe from one number of portions to another, add whole items (rounded up), allow for the part of a delivery that is lost in trimming, and work out how many packs to buy. At Expert+ you start from the stock in the kitchen, find how many portions it makes, how many packs are missing for a booking, and judge a colleague's order against both the stock and the budget.

## Service timing

Stages that follow each other add up, and the start time is found by counting back from service. At Hard and Expert, items made at the same time are compared and the longest sets the start. At Expert+ food is baked in batches, so you find the number of batches, the latest start and how long the first batch has to wait, then check both against the plan.

## Dish cost and gross profit

Price per kilogram and the weight used give each ingredient's cost, the batch cost and the cost per portion. Then gross profit percentage at a menu price, and the price that reaches a target. At Expert+ you work backwards from a price and target to the largest food cost, the largest price per kilogram for the main ingredient, and two supplier quotes.

## Stock and waste

Opening stock plus deliveries minus closing stock is what was used. Portions sold times grams per portion is what should have been used. The gap is waste, shown as a weight, a percentage and a cost. At Expert+ a stated waste tolerance gives the most stock that may be used and the largest delivery that stays within it.
