## General

Kitchen scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (g, kg, minutes, clock times, $).

1. Read the stated rules first: yield, pack sizes, plating or hold times, the target gross profit and the waste tolerance.
2. Convert units before you calculate: 1 kg = 1000 g; 60 minutes = 1 hour; price per kg × kilograms = cost.
3. Round only as each part asks. Counts of packs, eggs and batches round up; whole portions that can be made round down.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Scaling, yield and buying

Weight per portion = recipe weight ÷ recipe portions. Weight for M portions = weight per portion × M. Whole items (eggs) are scaled the same way and rounded up.

If only y% of what you buy is usable, raw weight to buy = usable weight ÷ (y ÷ 100). Packs = raw weight ÷ pack size, rounded up.

Working backwards: usable stock = packs × size × yield. Portions it makes = usable stock ÷ grams per portion, rounded down. For a booking, raw weight needed = covers × grams per portion ÷ yield; take away the stock and divide what is missing by the pack size, rounding up.

## Service timing

Stages that follow one another add up: prep + cook + rest. Latest start = service time − total time. Borrow 60 minutes when the minutes go below zero.

When items are made at the same time, work out each total. The longest one sets the start of the whole job, and the plating time is taken off before that. To finish an item at the same moment as the others, start it its own total before that moment.

Batches = portions ÷ tray size, rounded up. Oven time = batches × bake time. The first tray comes out after one bake, so it waits (batches − 1) × bake time plus the plating time before service.

## Dish cost and gross profit

Ingredient cost = price per kg × grams ÷ 1000. Batch cost is the sum; cost per portion = batch cost ÷ portions.

Gross profit % = (price − food cost) ÷ price × 100. Working backwards, food cost = (100 − target)% of the price, so price = food cost ÷ (1 − target as a fraction). Round the price up when asked, so the target is still reached.

For a largest price per kg: largest batch cost − the other ingredients' cost = what the main ingredient may cost, then ÷ kilograms used.

## Stock and waste

Used = opening + deliveries − closing. Theoretical use = portions × grams ÷ 1000. Waste = used − theoretical; waste % = waste ÷ used × 100; cost of waste = waste kg × price per kg.

With a tolerance of w% of the stock used, theoretical use is at least (100 − w)% of the stock used, so used ≤ theoretical ÷ (1 − w ÷ 100). The largest delivery = that limit − opening + closing.
