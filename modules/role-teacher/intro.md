# Applied scenarios (Teacher)

Scenario questions for the **Teacher** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one shows a student's worked answer as numbered steps. One step holds the first mistake, and every later step follows correctly from the step above it, even when that step is wrong. You work like a marker or a tutor: find the first wrong step, give the right value, and carry on to the right answer.

Higher levels add the teacher's judgement. At Expert you award marks under a stated mark scheme, with or without follow-through. At Expert+ you also name the kind of error and the next thing to teach (Error type | Next step), and the marks depend on that kind: an arithmetic slip keeps its method mark, any other error does not. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Every number is exact, so no rounding is needed. Relative atomic masses are stated in each chemistry question. The mark schemes here are made up for practice and are not any real exam board's rules.

## Marking equation solutions

Linear equations with a bracket, a negative x coefficient, or a taxi fare, worked line by line. Marks go to each line that follows from the one above.

## Marking fractions, percentages and ratios

Fractions of a quantity, a rise then a fall in a price, and a share in a ratio. Watch for the wrong whole: a second percentage applies to the new price, not the original.

## Diagnosing data and motion errors

A mean from a frequency table, a speed from metres and minutes, and a percentage change. The mistake may be a slip, a wrong divisor or a unit conversion, and each points to a different next step.

## Diagnosing mole calculations

Moles to the mass of a product, a concentration to a mass of solute, and a mass to a concentration. Unit conversions between cm³ and dm³ are a common source of error.
