## General

In every scenario the student's lines are meant to follow each other. Your job is to find the first line that does not.

1. Redo each line from the line above it, using the student's own number, not the true one.
2. The first line that does not follow is the wrong step. Later lines are consistent with it, so do not blame them.
3. The corrected value is what that line should have been, given the line above it.
4. The right answer comes from working the whole problem correctly from the start.
5. For marks, read the stated scheme exactly: weights per line, whether follow-through is allowed, and whether the final answer earns marks.
6. For the diagnosis, compare the operation the student wrote with the one the line needed. Right operation, wrong value is a slip; anything else changed the operation.

## Marking equation solutions

Undo the operations in reverse order. A negative coefficient means dividing by a negative. Adding instead of subtracting is a sign error; multiplying instead of dividing is a wrong operation.

With follow-through, only the wrong line loses its marks. Without it, the wrong line and every later line lose theirs.

## Marking fractions, percentages and ratios

Over a common denominator, the numerators add. A second percentage applies to the price after the first change. For a ratio, one part is the total divided by the number of parts, and a share is a multiple of one part.

## Diagnosing data and motion errors

A mean from a table divides the sum of frequency × value by the sum of the frequencies, not by the number of rows. For speed, put distance in km and time in hours first. Percentage change divides the change by the starting value.

## Diagnosing mole calculations

Moles = mass ÷ molar mass. Moles = concentration × volume in dm³, and 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³. Use the equation's ratio to move between substances.
