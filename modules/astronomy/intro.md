# Astronomy

Work out how far, how fast and how big things are in space. Every question states the constants and planet or star values it uses, so you never need to remember them. They are rounded textbook values:

| Constant | Value used |
| --- | --- |
| Speed of light, c | 3.00 × 10⁵ km/s (300 000 km/s) |
| 1 astronomical unit (AU) | 1.50 × 10⁸ km (150 million km) |
| 1 light-year (ly) | 9.46 × 10¹² km |
| 1 year | 3.16 × 10⁷ s |
| 1 day | 86 400 s |

**Topics:** light travel times and unit changes; Kepler's third law (T² = a³ with T in years and a in AU) and orbital speed (2πr ÷ T); shrinking the solar system into a scale model; how big things look (small-angle rule) and surface gravity compared with Earth.

**Answer formats**

- Type plain numbers. Every question says how to round; within half a step counts (±0.005 for 2 decimal places).
- Very big answers ask for **A × 10ⁿ**: the question tells you n, and you type just A (for example `4.01`).
- Angles are in degrees or arcseconds (1° = 3600″), as the question says.

**Expert** questions are multi-step problems in several parts: parallax and parsecs, the shortest and longest radio delay to another planet, weighing a star from a planet's orbit, comet ellipses (forwards and backwards), scale models fixed by the farthest planet or by the size of the model Earth, total versus annular eclipses, and surface gravity from G × M ÷ R² (and back).

> [!TIP]
> Convert to the same units (km and seconds) before you divide. The calculator understands `3e5`, `10^6` and `pi`.
