# Accounting Terms: how to solve

## General
- Ask first: is this a **point in time** (balance sheet) or a **period** (income statement, cash flow)?
- Sort every item into one of five buckets: asset, liability, equity, revenue or expense.
- Every transaction touches at least two places, and **Assets = Liabilities + Equity** must still balance afterwards.
- For numeric answers, type the number only, with no currency sign or commas.

## Financial statements
1. **Balance sheet**: a snapshot on one date. Balances of assets, liabilities and equity live here.
2. **Income statement**: revenues and expenses over a period, ending in net income.
3. **Cash flow statement**: real cash in and out over a period, split into three sections.
4. Pick the cash flow section by asking who is on the other side (see the list below).
5. Net income links the statements: it flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet.

- Day-to-day customers, suppliers, staff → **operating**
- Buying or selling long-term assets (equipment, buildings) → **investing**
- Owners and lenders (shares, loans, dividends) → **financing**

Watch out: a loan's *interest* is usually operating, but repaying the loan's *principal* is financing.

## Assets and liabilities
1. **Asset**: something the company owns or controls that brings future benefit (cash, inventory, receivables, machinery).
2. **Liability**: something the company owes (payables, loans, customer prepayments it still has to earn).
3. **Current vs non-current**: current means used up or due within **one year**; anything longer is non-current.
4. Prepaid items (paid now, used later) are assets; money received before the work is done is a liability (unearned revenue).
5. A **contra** account sits under another account and reduces it, like accumulated depreciation under equipment.

Watch out: a long-term loan splits in two: the slice due in the next 12 months is a current liability.

## The accounting equation
1. Write **A = L + E** and fill in the two numbers you know.
2. Rearrange for the missing one: E = A − L, L = A − E.
3. For a transaction, list the two accounts it changes and the direction of each.
4. Check the equation still balances: both sides move together, or one side swaps between two items of the same kind.

Example: assets 500, liabilities 200 → equity = 500 − 200 = 300.

Watch out: buying equipment for cash swaps one asset for another, so total assets do not change.

## Key terms
1. Spot the formula words: **gross profit** = revenue − cost of goods sold; **net profit** subtracts *all* expenses too.
2. **Working capital** = current assets − current liabilities.
3. **Straight-line depreciation** per year = (cost − salvage value) ÷ useful life.
4. **Accrual accounting**: record revenue when *earned* and expenses when *incurred*, not when cash moves.
5. **Matching principle**: put expenses in the same period as the revenue they helped earn.

Example: cost 1,000, salvage 200, 4 years → (1000 − 200) ÷ 4 = 200 per year.

Watch out: an *accrued* expense has happened but is not yet paid; a *prepaid* expense is paid but not yet used.
