## General

Retail scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the bases straight: markup uses the cost, margin uses the selling price, shrinkage is a share of sales.

1. Read the stated definitions and rules first: markup and margin, the staffing rule, the deal wording, the tolerance or target.
2. Percentage of what? Say the base before you divide (cost, selling price, sales, full price).
3. Round only as each part asks. When a count must be "round up", a part-person or part-item still needs a whole one.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule. Decide "at least" or "no more than" from the wording.

## Footfall and staffing

Purchases = visitors × conversion rate ÷ 100. Sales = purchases × average basket. Sales per hour = sales ÷ opening hours. Staff needed = sales per hour ÷ the most one person may be rostered for, rounded up. Pay = staff × hours × hourly wage; as a percentage of sales, ÷ sales × 100.

To work backwards: purchases = sales target ÷ basket, and visitors = purchases ÷ (conversion ÷ 100), rounded up. A rota meets the rule if it has at least the staff you found.

## Markup and margin

Price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100). Profit = price − cost. Margin = profit ÷ price × 100; markup = profit ÷ cost × 100. The same profit gives a smaller margin than markup.

From a target margin, cost is the rest of the price: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100), and cost = price × (1 − margin ÷ 100). A supplier rise raises the cost, not the price, so the margin falls: recompute profit ÷ the unchanged price. Items to cover a fixed cost = fixed cost ÷ profit per item, rounded up.

## Deals and sale prices

Price the whole basket item by item. 3 for 2: every third item is free. Buy 2 get the 3rd half price: the third item in each group of three costs half. Buy 1 get the 2nd half price: the second item in each pair costs half. A percentage off applies to every item. "Any 2 for $Y" charges $Y per pair and full price for a leftover item.

Compare the totals and take the lowest. The shop's margin on a sale = (amount paid − what the items cost the shop) ÷ amount paid × 100. Working backwards, divide the total paid by the number of items actually paid for to get the unit price. A deal's saving as a percentage of full price = free value ÷ full value × 100.

## Stock turn and shrinkage

Expected closing stock = opening + purchases − sales (all at cost). Shrinkage = expected − counted. Shrinkage % = shrinkage ÷ sales × 100. Average stock = (opening + closing) ÷ 2, and stock turn = sales ÷ average stock.

Working backwards: shrinkage = sales × shrinkage % ÷ 100, then counted stock = opening + purchases − sales − shrinkage. For a tolerance, "within" means no more than it; for a target turn, "met" means at least it.
