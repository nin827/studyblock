# Applied scenarios (Archaeologist)

Expert+ questions for the **Archaeologist** role. Each one is a single made-up scenario with several linked answers, and every part is worked out from **figures stated in the question**: a sample's carbon-14 percentage, the relations between layers, coin years, trench sizes and sherd counts. You never need to recall a real site or date; you need to reason with the numbers. Where a rule matters (half-life, what a terminus post quem is, what EVE means) it is stated in the prompt, and each part says how to round.

Years before AD 1 are written "BC" and count **down** towards AD 1. There is **no year 0**: 1 BC is followed directly by AD 1. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

## Radiocarbon dating

A sample has a stated percentage of its carbon-14 left, and the half-life is given. Work out how many half-lives have passed, the sample's age, and the calendar year it died. At higher levels you compare two samples, find the gap between them, and check a result against the order of the layers.

## Stratigraphy and coins

Statements such as "lies over" and "is cut by" fix the order of site contexts, and coins give each context a terminus post quem: the earliest year it can date from. Find the order, the TPQ of a chosen context, and at the top tiers how many contexts are later, whether a date is possible, and how long the window is to a dated floor.

## Trench finds

Work from a trench's length, width and depth to its area and volume, the density of finds per m² or per m³, and the days a team of diggers needs. At the top tiers you compare two spits and work backwards from a density to the volume, the depth and the finds expected in a neighbouring trench.

## Pottery counts

Sort sherds by fabric, find the total and each fabric's percentage, and add up rim arcs into estimated vessel equivalents (EVE). At the top tier you start from one fabric's count and percentage, share out the rest in a ratio and see how a second season changes the picture.
