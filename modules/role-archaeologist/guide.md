## General

Archaeological reasoning here is careful arithmetic on the figures in the question. Work in order, keep units (years, m, m², m³, sherds) attached, and read each part's rounding instruction.

1. Read the stated rules first: the half-life, what TPQ means, how EVE is counted, how days are rounded.
2. A later part often uses an earlier answer, so check that one before carrying on. All the answers here are exact whole numbers or exact 2 dp figures.
3. BC years count down: 400 BC is earlier than 300 BC. There is no year 0, so a span from BC to AD is one year shorter than adding the two year numbers.
4. For a Yes/No or "which" part, find the numbers first, then compare them with the stated rule.

## Radiocarbon dating

Each half-life halves the carbon-14 left: 100%, 50%, 25%, 12.5%, 6.25%. Count the halvings to reach the stated percentage, then age = half-lives × the stated half-life.

To find the year the living thing died, count back from the year of the test by the age. Going from AD into BC passes the missing year 0, so the BC year is one more than a plain subtraction suggests.

Two samples tested in the same year differ in age by the difference of their half-lives. Less carbon-14 left means older. Working from a year back to a percentage: age = the span between the death year and the test year (allow for the missing year 0), then halve once per half-life.

## Stratigraphy and coins

"X lies over Y", "Y is cut by X" and "Y is sealed beneath X" all mean X is later than Y. Chain the statements, starting from the context that is never later than another, to get the order from oldest to newest.

The TPQ of a context is the year of the newest coin in that context or in any earlier one, so it never goes backwards as you go up the section. A context can inherit its TPQ from a coin below it. A context can be dated no earlier than its TPQ.

A dated floor at the top gives a window: the span in years from a context's TPQ to the floor's year, allowing for the missing year 0 across BC and AD.

## Trench finds

Area = length × width, and volume = area × depth. Density = finds ÷ area (per m²) or finds ÷ volume (per m³).

Days of digging = volume ÷ (diggers × rate per digger per day), rounded up because a part day counts as a whole day. For several spits, each has its own volume and density; compare densities, not totals.

Working backwards: volume = finds ÷ density, depth = volume ÷ area, and a neighbouring trench at the same density and depth gives density × its area × the depth.

## Pottery counts

Total = the sum of the fabric counts. Percentage of a fabric = its count ÷ the total × 100.

EVE (estimated vessel equivalents) = the total of the rim percentages ÷ 100. Compare fabrics by their EVE totals.

Working backwards from a count and a percentage: total = count × 100 ÷ percentage. The sherds not in that fabric are shared in the stated ratio (divide by the sum of the ratio parts, then multiply by each part). When a season adds sherds, add them to the right fabric and the total before finding the new percentage.
