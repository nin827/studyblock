# Mechanisms

How machines trade force for distance and speed for torque: levers, gears, pulleys, belt drives, and the power that passes through them.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| effort × effort distance = load × load distance | a balanced lever (distances from the pivot) |
| MA = load ÷ effort | mechanical advantage: how much the force is multiplied |
| VR = distance moved by effort ÷ distance moved by load | velocity ratio; for an ideal machine MA = VR |
| efficiency = MA ÷ VR × 100 % | the same as work out ÷ work in |
| gear ratio = driven teeth ÷ driver teeth | = input speed ÷ output speed |
| teeth × rpm (gears), diameter × rpm (belts) | the same on both sides of a mesh or a belt |
| pulley system: MA = VR = number of supporting rope sections | when ideal |
| P = T × ω, ω = 2π × rpm ÷ 60 | power in W, torque in N·m, ω in rad/s |

**Conventions:** a machine is ideal (no friction, light parts) unless an efficiency is given. Gear ratio is driven teeth ÷ driver teeth, so a ratio above 1 slows the output and raises its torque. Belts don't slip. Efficiencies in series multiply. Where g is needed it is 10 N/kg. Every question states its units and rounding.

> [!TIP]
> Speed and torque swap: if a stage makes the output turn 3 times slower, it makes the torque 3 times bigger (less any losses).

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | One rule: a balanced lever, a gear or belt speed, rope pulled, P = Tω |
| Medium | Efficiency, teeth or pulley size for a target speed, idler gears, rpm to power |
| Hard | MA, VR and efficiency from a test, compound gear trains, gearboxes with losses |
| Expert | Working backwards: size a lever arm or pick a gear pair, the fewest rope sections, sizing a motor, a belt and gear chain |
