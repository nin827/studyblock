# Gases & Pressure

Practise pressure in all its forms: a heel pressing on the floor, water pushing on a diver, gas squeezed in a syringe and a small force lifting a car in a hydraulic jack. Every constant you need (g, densities, atmospheric pressure) is given in the question.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `P = F / A` | pressure (Pa = N/m²) = force / area |
| `P = ρ × g × h` | pressure due to a liquid at depth h (g = 9.8 N/kg) |
| `p1 × V1 = p2 × V2` | Boyle's law (constant temperature) |
| `V1 / T1 = V2 / T2` | Charles's law (constant pressure), T in kelvin |
| `p1 / T1 = p2 / T2` | pressure law (constant volume), T in kelvin |
| `p1 × V1 / T1 = p2 × V2 / T2` | combined gas law |
| `F1 / A1 = F2 / A2` | hydraulics: the same pressure everywhere in the liquid |
| `P = P_atm + ρ × g × h` | total pressure under a liquid surface open to the air |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Whole numbers in m², Pa and K, whole-number answers |
| Medium | Areas in cm² or mm², depths in cm, pressures in kPa, temperatures in °C; still whole-number answers |
| Hard | Decimals, combined gas law, circular pistons, forces on windows; answers rounded to 2 decimal places, some in several parts |

**Tip:** 1 m² = 10 000 cm² = 1 000 000 mm², 1 kPa = 1000 Pa, and K = °C + 273. In the gas laws the pressure and volume units just carry through, but temperatures must be in kelvin. The calculator starts with the question's numbers; a label like `_A_cm2` or `_T1_C` shows the unit of that value.
