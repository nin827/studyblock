# Biology: how to solve

## General
- Link each structure to its **job**: most questions describe a job and ask for the name.
- Look for clue words in the question ("green", "iron-containing", "away from the heart") and match them to one part.
- Check the requested form: one word, two words, a ratio like `3:1`, or a fraction like `1/2`.
- Spelling counts for typed answers; British spellings (haemoglobin) follow the question.

## Cells
1. Ask what the structure *does*, then name it (see the table below).
2. Plant-only parts: **cell wall** (cellulose), chloroplasts, large permanent vacuole.
3. **Prokaryotic** cells (bacteria) have no nucleus; **eukaryotic** cells do ("eu" = true nucleus).
4. Osmosis is about **water** moving through a membrane from dilute to concentrated.

| Job | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| control centre, holds DNA | nucleus |
| releases energy (respiration) | mitochondria |
| photosynthesis | chloroplast (contains chlorophyll) |
| makes proteins | ribosomes |
| packages and ships proteins | Golgi apparatus |
| digests worn-out parts | lysosomes |

Watch out: the cell *membrane* controls what enters and leaves; the cell *wall* gives support.

## Human body
1. Find the organ system first (circulatory, respiratory, digestive, excretory, hormonal), then the part.
2. Blood: red cells carry oxygen (using **haemoglobin**), white cells fight infection, **platelets** clot.
3. Vessels: **A**rteries carry blood **A**way from the heart; veins return it.
4. The heart's **left** side pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body, so its wall is thicker.
5. Hormones: insulin *lowers* blood glucose; glucagon raises it. Both come from the pancreas.

Watch out: gas exchange happens in the alveoli, not the bronchi.

## Genetics
1. Know the ladder: DNA → gene (a section that codes for a protein) → chromosome. Humans have 23 pairs.
2. Base pairs: **A–T** and **C–G** (think "Apple Tree", "Car Garage").
3. For a cross, draw a 2 × 2 Punnett square: one parent's alleles across the top, the other's down the side.
4. Count the boxes. Genotype ratio counts AA : Aa : aa; phenotype ratio counts "shows dominant" vs "shows recessive".
5. Vocabulary: homozygous = two identical alleles; heterozygous = two different; phenotype = what you can see.

Example: Aa × aa gives Aa, Aa, aa, aa, so half the offspring are Aa.

Watch out: a recessive trait only shows with **two** recessive alleles (aa).

## Ecology
1. Follow the arrows: they show the **flow of energy**, from eaten to eater.
2. Name each level: producer (plant) → primary consumer (herbivore) → secondary consumer.
3. Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light needed).
4. Aerobic respiration is the reverse: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.
5. Levels of grouping: population (one species) → community (all species) → ecosystem (plus the non-living parts).

Watch out: decomposers break down *dead* material; they are not the same as consumers.

## Skeleton
1. Place the bone: head, chest, arm, hip or leg.
2. Upper limb: humerus (upper arm), then radius and ulna (forearm; the radius is on the thumb side).
3. Lower limb: femur (thigh), then tibia and fibula (lower leg; the tibia is the shinbone).
4. Remember the record holders: 206 bones in an adult, longest is the femur, smallest is the stapes in the middle ear.

Everyday names have anatomical twins: kneecap = patella, collarbone = clavicle, shoulder blade = scapula, breastbone = sternum, shinbone = tibia.

Example: "the bone between the shoulder and the elbow" is the humerus.

Watch out: the clavicle (collarbone) is at the front; the scapula (shoulder blade) is at the back.

## Organs and body systems
1. Decide which job is described: pumping, breathing, digesting, filtering, controlling or covering.
2. Follow food in order: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (absorbs nutrients), large intestine (absorbs water).
3. Helpers: the liver makes bile, the gallbladder stores it. Kidneys filter blood into urine; the ureters carry it to the bladder.
4. For a "which system" question, ask what the part does, then name the system that does that job (see the table below).
5. Heart: two atria on top receive blood, two ventricles below pump it out; valves stop backflow.
6. Route: body, venae cavae, right atrium, right ventricle, pulmonary artery, lungs, pulmonary veins, left atrium, left ventricle, aorta, body.

Example: the thyroid gland releases hormones, so it is in the endocrine system.

| System | Main parts | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circulatory | heart, blood, blood vessels | carries oxygen and nutrients |
| Respiratory | trachea, bronchi, lungs | gas exchange |
| Digestive | stomach, intestines, liver | breaks down and absorbs food |
| Nervous | brain, spinal cord, nerves | carries signals |
| Skeletal | bones, joints | support and protection |
| Muscular | muscles | movement |
| Urinary | kidneys, ureters, bladder | removes waste as urine |
| Endocrine | thyroid, pituitary, other glands | hormones |
| Immune and lymphatic | lymph nodes, white blood cells | fights infection |
| Integumentary | skin, hair, nails | outer covering |

Watch out: the skin is the largest organ overall, but the liver is the largest organ inside the body. The pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood, and the pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood.
