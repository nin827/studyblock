## General

Underwriting scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight ($, %, points, counts).

1. Read the stated rules first: the rating table, the order of loadings and discounts, the excess, limit and co-insurance, the score limits, the caps.
2. Write down which number each part asks for, and its rounding.
3. Percentage steps are applied in the order stated, each to the figure before it, not all to the original.
4. For a yes or no, find the numbers first, then compare using the stated rule. Equal to the limit counts as within it, unless the rule says "more than" or "above".

## Premium rating

Rated premium = base premium × the factor for each band that applies (read the band from the applicant's age, location and claims). A loading of l% multiplies by (100 + l) ÷ 100, a discount of d% by (100 − d) ÷ 100, in the order stated. A minimum premium is a floor: charge the higher of the two.

Change on last year (%) = (new − last year) ÷ last year × 100. With two cars, price each, add them, apply the household discount, then the instalment surcharge, then divide by 12.

## Loss and combined ratio

Expected claims = policies × chance of a claim. Claims cost = expected claims × average claim. Loss ratio = claims cost ÷ premiums × 100, where premiums = policies × average premium.

Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio. Under 100% means an underwriting profit. Underwriting result = premiums − claims − expenses.

To break even on a target combined ratio: affordable loss ratio = target − expense ratio; premium needed = expected claim per policy ÷ affordable loss ratio, rounded up when asked. Rate rise = (needed − current) ÷ current × 100.

## Excess, limits and claims

For each loss: take off the excess (not below zero), take the co-insurance percentage of what is left (100% if none), then cap at the limit. The policyholder bears the rest of the loss. Add the losses up one at a time.

An annual aggregate limit is applied last, to the total of the insurer's payments for the year.

## Risk scorecard and exposure

Score = the sum of the points for the band that applies to each factor. Compare with the limits: at or below the Accept limit is Accept, up to the Refer limit is Refer, above it is Decline.

Exposure after writing = exposure already held + this sum insured. A proposal that is not written adds nothing for the proposals after it. Follow the stated action rule: only an Accept can be written, and only if the exposure fits under the cap.
