# Accounting Theory

The rules behind the books: which accounts a transaction debits and credits, the concepts that decide how items are recorded, where each item belongs, and how every transaction keeps **Assets = Liabilities + Equity** in balance. The firms are made up, and no country-specific law or tax is involved.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Double entry | Pick the account debited and the account credited for a transaction; Expert balances a ledger account |
| Accounting concepts | Name the concept (accruals, prudence, consistency, going concern, business entity, materiality) and the right treatment, or spot which one a mistake breaks |
| Classifying items | Statement and category of an item, including the 12-month rule for current and non-current |
| The accounting equation | Say what a transaction does to total assets, liabilities and equity; Expert runs three transactions |
| Journal entries | Write the whole entry, Account, Debit and Credit; Hard and Expert are compound entries of 3 or 4 lines |

**Conventions used here**

- Goods bought for resale go to **Inventory**. Sales record the **revenue only**; the cost of the goods sold is left out.
- Goods for resale bought on credit are owed in **Trade payables**; anything else bought on credit (equipment, vans) in **Other payables**.
- *Cash* means the business bank account. Depreciation is credited to **Accumulated depreciation**, and the owner's withdrawals go to **Drawings**.
- The accruals and matching ideas are treated as one concept, **Accruals (matching)**.

**Answer formats.** Most answers are built from blocks, one per column (for example *Debit* and *Credit*); you can switch to **Choose** and pick a whole row. Numbers are typed without `$` or commas.

**Journal entries: type or drop blocks.** Type straight into any cell (account names are suggested as you type), or drag an account or amount block onto a cell. You can also tap a cell and then a block, or a block and then a cell. Blocks can be used more than once. Put each amount in the Debit *or* the Credit column, leave spare rows empty, and use **+ Row** if you need another line. Lines can be in any order.
