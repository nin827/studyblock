# Accounting Theory: how to solve

## General

- Every transaction changes at least two accounts. Name them first, then decide whether each goes up or down.
- Type of account → its normal side:

| Account type | Increase | Decrease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset, expense, drawings | Debit | Credit |
| Liability, capital, income | Credit | Debit |

## Double entry

1. Write the two accounts that change, with ↑ or ↓.
2. Use the table above to put each on the debit or the credit side.
3. Check: one debit and one credit, for the same amount.

Example: paid rent of $500 → rent expense ↑ (debit), cash ↓ (credit).

**Watch out:** returns, write-offs and goods taken by the owner reduce an asset, so that asset is **credited**. Sales returns has its own account, debited.

**Expert.** List only the transactions that touch the account asked about, put each amount on its side, add any opening balance, and take the difference. The balance sits on the bigger side.

## Accounting concepts

- **Accruals (matching):** record income and expenses in the period they are earned or used, not when cash moves; spread a long-lived asset's cost over the years it helps earn revenue.
- **Prudence:** do not overstate assets or profit: write off bad debts, value inventory at the lower amount, do not record hoped-for income.
- **Consistency:** keep the same methods from year to year unless something real changes.
- **Going concern:** assume the business carries on, so value assets at cost less depreciation. If it is closing, use break-up values instead.
- **Business entity:** the owner's personal spending is drawings, and the owner paying a business bill personally is extra capital.
- **Materiality:** tiny amounts need not be treated precisely (a cheap stapler is simply expensed).

For a mistake, ask what the firm should have done instead; the rule that says so is the concept broken.

## Classifying items

1. Balances on a date (assets, liabilities, equity) go on the **balance sheet**; income and expenses over the year go on the **income statement**.
2. Owns or is owed → asset. Owes → liability. Owner's stake or kept profits → equity.
3. **12-month rule:** due or used within 12 months is current; longer is non-current.

## The accounting equation

1. Find what happens to each account, then add up by total: assets, liabilities, equity.
2. Income and capital raise equity; expenses, drawings and sales returns lower it.
3. Two asset accounts moving in opposite directions leave total assets unchanged.
4. Check: change in assets = change in liabilities + change in equity.

**Expert.** Opening assets = opening liabilities + opening equity. Apply each transaction's effect in turn, and check the closing totals still balance.
