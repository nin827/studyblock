# Geometry

Measure shapes and solids: perimeter and area, angles in triangles and polygons, the Pythagorean theorem, and volume. Lengths are in cm, areas in cm², volumes in cm³ and angles in degrees. Some questions are multiple choice, with wrong options taken from common mistakes.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `A = l × w`, `P = 2 × (l + w)` | area and perimeter of a rectangle |
| `A = ½ × b × h` | area of a triangle with base b and perpendicular height h |
| `A = π × r²`, `C = 2 × π × r = π × d` | area and circumference of a circle |
| `a + b + c = 180°` | angles in a triangle |
| `90°` / `180°` | complementary / supplementary angles add up to this |
| `(n - 2) × 180°` | sum of the interior angles of an n-sided polygon |
| `(n - 2) × 180° / n` | each interior angle of a regular polygon |
| `a² + b² = c²` | Pythagoras: c is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle |
| `V = l × w × h` | volume of a cuboid |
| `V = π × r² × h` | volume of a cylinder |
| `V = 4/3 × π × r³` | volume of a sphere |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Whole-number sides, triangle angles, Pythagorean triples like 3, 4, 5 |
| Medium | Circles, polygons, finding a missing leg, cuboids, cylinders and spheres |
| Hard | Decimal lengths, a circle's radius from its area, sides of a regular polygon, unknown polygon angles |
| Expert | Multi-step problems with several answers: see below |

**Expert** combines ideas and often works backwards: composite shapes (a rectangle with a semicircle), a rectangle from its perimeter and area, paths around a lawn, polygons from a relation between interior and exterior angles, angles written in terms of x or in a ratio, 3D diagonals of a box, a slipping ladder, triangles on a grid (is it right-angled?), tanks in litres, solids built from a cylinder and a cone or hemisphere (the formula is given), pouring water between containers, and similar solids. Each part says how to round.

**Tip:** when a question involves π, type `pi` in the calculator rather than 3.14, and round to 2 decimal places (answers within 0.005 are accepted). Watch for a diameter where you need a radius: halve it first. Type angles as plain numbers without the ° sign.
