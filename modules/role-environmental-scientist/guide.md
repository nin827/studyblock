## General

Environmental scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (m³/s, mg/L, kg, tonnes, mm, hectares, m³).

1. Read the stated rules first: the mixing rule, the emission factors, the sample-size rule, the runoff conversion.
2. Write down which number each part asks for, and its rounding.
3. Whole things (samples, credits, tanks, days) round **up** when you need enough, and **down** when you ask how many whole days something lasts.
4. For a verdict, find the numbers first, then compare them with the stated limit, guideline or target.

## Pollutant mixing at an outfall

Mixed concentration = (river flow × river concentration + outfall flow × outfall concentration) ÷ (river flow + outfall flow). It always lies between the two concentrations, nearer the one with more flow.

Load (kg/day) = flow (m³/s) × concentration (mg/L) × 86.4. Loads from different sources add. The outfall's share of the load = its load ÷ the total load.

With a second outfall, the water leaving the first mix is the "river" for the second one: use the combined flow and the first mixed concentration. "At or below" the limit is within it; strictly above exceeds it.

## Carbon footprint and offsets

Emissions = activity × emission factor, in kg; divide by 1000 for tonnes. A source's share = its tonnes ÷ the total tonnes × 100.

A reduction % = (old total − new total) ÷ old total × 100. A target "x% below today" gives an allowed total = today's total × (1 − x ÷ 100).

Offsets cover the gap above the allowed total: credits = gap ÷ tonnes per credit, rounded **up** to a whole credit; the cost is credits × price.

## Sampling plans and guidelines

Mean = the sum of the readings ÷ how many there are. Sample standard deviation: square each reading's distance from the mean, add, divide by (n − 1), square root.

Samples needed n = (2 × σ ÷ E)², rounded up. A fixed fee plus a cost per sample gives the survey cost. Further samples = n − the number already taken.

An area-weighted mean = Σ(area × mean) ÷ total area, not a plain average of the zone means.

## Rainfall, runoff and water storage

1 ha = 10 000 m², and 1 mm of rain on 1 m² is 1 litre, so 1 ha × 1 mm = 10 m³ and 1000 m²·mm = 1 m³. Runoff = area × depth × runoff coefficient.

A capture percentage keeps that share of the runoff. Whole days of supply = captured volume ÷ daily use, rounded down. Tanks needed = total volume ÷ tank size, rounded up.

For monthly storage, find each month's harvest, then keep a running total of (harvest − demand) starting at 0. The storage needed is how far below zero the running total ever goes. The share of demand met = total harvest ÷ total demand × 100.
