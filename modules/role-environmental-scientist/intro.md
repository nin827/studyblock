# Applied scenarios (Environmental Scientist)

Scenario questions for the **Environmental Scientist** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: flow-weighted averages, percentages, standard deviation, rounding up to whole samples or credits, and comparing a result with a stated limit. A later part often builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Every factor, limit and rule is stated in the question, and each part says how to round.

## Pollutant mixing at an outfall

A treatment works discharges into a river, and the two flows mix. You find the flow-weighted concentration below the outfall, the daily load of the substance, and whether the river stays within a stated limit. At Expert+ a second outfall sits further downstream, and you decide which sites go over the limit.

## Carbon footprint and offsets

Emissions come from activity times an emission factor (all made up). You add up electricity, vehicles and gas, find each source's share, and test a plan against a target cut. At Expert+ you work out what is still above the target and how many whole offset credits cover it, and what they cost.

## Sampling plans and guidelines

Readings from a site give a mean, a count above a guideline and a standard deviation. A stated rule turns the spread and a margin into the number of samples needed, and then into a survey cost. At Expert+ two zones are weighted by area, and you find how many further samples are still to take.

## Rainfall, runoff and water storage

Rain on a catchment becomes runoff according to area, depth and a runoff coefficient. You size a pond or tanks, work out how long captured water lasts, and at Expert+ track a tank month by month to find the storage needed.
