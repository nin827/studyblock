# Exponents & Logarithms

An exponent (or index) says how many times to multiply a number by itself: `2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32`. A logarithm runs it backwards: `log₂ 32 = 5` asks "2 to what power gives 32?". Together they describe anything that grows or shrinks by the same factor each step, from savings to radioactive samples.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Index laws | Combine powers, and evaluate negative and fractional indices like `2⁻³` and `8^(2/3)` |
| Logarithms | Evaluate logs, switch between log and index form, and use the log laws |
| Solving equations | Solve `aˣ = b` exactly by matching bases, or with logs and rounding |
| Growth & decay | Compound interest, depreciation, doubling, half-life, and how long things take |

**Answer formats**

- **Exact answers** are a whole number or a fraction in simplest form, typed like `3/8`, `-1/2` or `9/4` (an improper fraction, not a mixed number). `6/16` counts as wrong when the answer is `3/8`.
- **Rounded answers** say "Round to 2 decimal places"; answers within 0.005 count. Money is in dollars, typed without the `$`.
- Powers and log statements in the options are written like `2⁸`, `x⁻³`, `8^(2/3)` and `log₂ 32 = 5`.
- In the calculator, `log` is base 10, `ln` is base e, and `^` raises to a power. For another base, use `log(x) / log(b)`.
