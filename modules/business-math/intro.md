# Business Math

Everyday numbers for running a business: percentages, interest on savings and loans, and the difference between a profit margin and a markup. Amounts are in dollars.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `part = p / 100 × whole` | p percent of an amount |
| `change % = (new - old) / old × 100` | percent increase (positive) or decrease (negative) |
| `I = P × r × t` | simple interest on principal P at rate r for t years |
| `A = P × (1 + r)^t` | compound interest, added once a year |
| `markup % = profit / cost × 100` | profit measured against the cost |
| `margin % = profit / price × 100` | profit measured against the selling price |
| `price = cost × (1 + markup)` | selling price from a markup |

Rates r are written as decimals in the formulas, so 5% becomes 0.05.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Round numbers and whole-number answers |
| Medium | Any numbers, reverse percentages and compound interest |
| Hard | Amounts with cents, decimal rates and target margins |

> [!TIP]
> Type percent answers as plain numbers without the % sign, for example `12.5`. Round to 2 decimal places when asked (answers within 0.005 are accepted). The same profit gives a smaller margin than markup, because the price is bigger than the cost.
