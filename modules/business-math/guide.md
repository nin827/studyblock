# How to solve: Business Math

## General

- Turn a percent into a decimal by dividing by 100: 15% = 0.15.
- Type percent answers as plain numbers without the % sign, e.g. `12.5`.
- Easy answers are whole numbers. Medium and hard answers are rounded to 2 decimal places.
- Keep full precision until the last step, then round once.
- The given values (`_percent`, `_cost`, `_principal` …) are already in the calculator, so build each step on top of them.

## Percentages

1. **p% of an amount:** `p / 100 × amount`.
2. **Percent change:** `(new - old) / old × 100`. It's negative for a decrease.
3. **Original price after a change:** a 20% increase means new = original × 1.2, so original = new ÷ 1.2.

Example: a price going from $80 to $100 is a change of 20 / 80 × 100 = 25%.

**Watch out:** always divide by the **old** value. To undo a percent change, divide; don't take the percent off the new price.

## Interest

1. Write the rate as a decimal: 5% = 0.05.
2. **Simple interest:** `I = P × r × t`. The total is P + I.
3. **Compound interest (yearly):** `A = P × (1 + r)^t`. The interest is A − P.
4. **Rate from simple interest:** `r = I / (P × t)`, then × 100 for a percent.

Example: $1000 at 5% for 2 years gives $100 simple interest, but grows to 1000 × 1.05² = $1102.50 compounded.

**Watch out:** check whether the question asks for the interest or the total amount.

## Margins and markup

1. Profit = selling price − cost.
2. **Markup** = profit ÷ **cost** × 100. **Margin** = profit ÷ **price** × 100.
3. **Price from a markup:** `cost × (1 + markup)`.
4. **Price for a target margin:** `cost ÷ (1 - margin)`.

Example: cost $80, price $100. Markup = 20 / 80 = 25%, margin = 20 / 100 = 20%.

**Watch out:** the same profit gives a smaller margin than markup, because the price is bigger than the cost.
