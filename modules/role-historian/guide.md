## General

Historical reasoning here is careful arithmetic on years. Work in order and keep the era (BC or AD) attached to every year.

1. Put the years on one line. BC years count down: 400 BC is earlier than 300 BC, and every BC year is earlier than every AD year.
2. A span within one era is a subtraction. A span from BC to AD is the two year numbers added, **minus 1**, because there is no year 0.
3. Century n runs from year 100(n-1)+1 to year 100n, so the year 1200 is in the 12th century and 1201 is in the 13th. BC centuries are counted the same way backwards from 1 BC.
4. A later part usually uses an earlier answer, so check it before you carry on. All the answers here are exact whole numbers or exact 2 dp figures.

## Dating a source

A document cannot be older than any event it mentions, so the earliest possible year is the year of the **latest** event it mentions. A text that quotes it must be later, so the year of the quoting text is the latest possible year. The range is the span between them.

To say an event is too late to be mentioned, compare its year with the latest possible year. When the author says an event happened N years before writing, move forward N years from the event, skipping the missing year 0 if you cross from BC into AD. Then test each other fact against that year.

## Reigns and terms

Days between two dates: the start day is day 0. Add whole years first (365 days, or 366 across 29 February), then the remaining months and days.

A whole year is completed on each anniversary of the start date. Compare the month and day of the end date with the month and day of the start date: if the end comes earlier in the year, one year fewer is complete.

Weekdays repeat every 7 days, so take the remainder after dividing the days by 7. To work back from an end date, subtract the days and apply the same remainder rule going backwards.

## Object labels

Age = the opening year minus the year made, with the BC correction (one fewer than adding the two numbers). For two objects, the gap is the span between their years.

Galleries and rooms use the year ranges in the question. BC years are always before AD 500. For a year made "N years before an event", go back N years from the event, again skipping the missing year 0.

## Exhibit timelines

Order by year, not by the size of the number: 490 BC comes before 44 BC. The span is the earliest to the latest year.

On the wall, distance in cm = years apart ÷ years per cm. To find the scale, divide the years between two dated objects by the centimetres between them. To read a marker's year, multiply its distance from a dated object by the scale and move forward (right) or back (left) from that object's year.
