# How to solve: Arithmetic

## General

- The calculator is off here on purpose: work it out in your head or on paper.
- For big numbers, write them in columns with the ones digits lined up on the right.
- Check your answer with the opposite operation: `a - b = c` means `c + b = a`.
- Hard division asks for 2 decimal places. Answers within 0.005 are accepted.

## Addition

1. Small numbers: start from the bigger one and count on. Make a ten when you can (`8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3`).
2. Big numbers: line the numbers up by place value.
3. Add the ones column first. If it reaches 10 or more, write the ones digit and **carry 1** into the tens column.
4. Move left one column at a time, adding any carry.

Example: `47 + 38`. Ones: 7 + 8 = 15, write 5, carry 1. Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Answer 85.

**Watch out:** don't forget the carry, and don't drop it in the wrong column.

## Subtraction

1. Small differences: count up from the smaller number to the larger one (`52 - 47`: 47 → 50 is 3, 50 → 52 is 2, so 5).
2. Otherwise, write the bigger number on top and subtract column by column, starting with the ones.
3. If a top digit is smaller than the one below it, **borrow 1** from the next column: the top digit gains 10 and the next digit loses 1.
4. Hard level: if the second number is bigger, work out `bigger - smaller` and put a minus sign in front.

Example: `72 - 38`. Ones: 2 is less than 8, so borrow: 12 - 8 = 4. Tens: 6 - 3 = 3. Answer 34.

**Watch out:** subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger one in each column (`2 - 8` → `8 - 2`) gives a wrong answer. Borrow instead.

## Multiplication

1. Easy level: know your times tables up to 10 × 10. If one is missing, build it from a fact you know (`7 × 8 = 7 × 7 + 7`).
2. Split the bigger number into tens and ones, multiply each part, then add (`46 × 7 = 40 × 7 + 6 × 7 = 280 + 42`).
3. Hard level: split the two-digit number instead (`312 × 24 = 312 × 20 + 312 × 4`).
4. Add the partial products, lining up the place values.

**Watch out:** `312 × 20` is `312 × 2` with a 0 on the end. Don't forget the 0.

## Division

1. Turn it into a times-table question: `56 ÷ 7` means "what times 7 makes 56?"
2. For bigger answers, take out a chunk of 10s first (`360 ÷ 15`: 15 × 20 = 300, leaving 60 = 15 × 4, so 24).
3. Hard level: use long or short division. Find the whole-number part and the remainder.
4. Put a decimal point, bring down a 0, and keep dividing. Work out 3 decimal places, then round to 2.

**Watch out:** round to 2 decimal places from the third decimal digit (5 or more rounds up), not by cutting the digits off.
