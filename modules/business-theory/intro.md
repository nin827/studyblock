# Business Theory

The core ideas behind how a business is owned, who it affects, and how it plans. The firms are made up, and the forms of ownership are the general textbook versions: the exact rules differ from country to country.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Forms of ownership | Tell sole traders, partnerships and private and public limited companies apart; share profits, dividends and debts |
| Stakeholders | Sort internal and external stakeholders, name what each wants, and see who gains and who loses from a decision |
| SMART objectives | Check whether an objective is measurable, achievable and time-bound, and whether it was met |
| SWOT analysis | Place factors as strengths, weaknesses, opportunities or threats |
| PESTLE analysis | Sort outside changes into political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental |

**Answer formats**

- Many questions show options as blocks in columns, for example `Shareholders | Limited | Shares sold to the public`. Compare the options column by column: most differ in only one or two places.
- Money: type the number only, no `$`. Each question says how exact to be.
- Where a judgement needs a rule (such as when a target counts as achievable), the question states it.

**Levels.** Easy names one thing; Medium and Hard combine two or three facts, or add a calculation. **Expert** questions combine ideas: choose a form of ownership from the owners' needs and then work out a profit share or dividend, or classify an outside change with PESTLE and then decide whether it is an opportunity or a threat for two different firms.
