# Business Theory: how to solve

## General

- Read the whole scenario before choosing: the deciding clue is often in the last sentence.
- With block options, decide each column on its own, then find the option that matches all of them.

## Forms of ownership

| Form | Owned by | Liability | Shares |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sole trader | One person | Unlimited | No shares |
| Partnership | Two or more partners | Unlimited | No shares |
| Private limited company | Shareholders | Limited | Sold privately |
| Public limited company | Shareholders | Limited | Sold to the public |

1. **Unlimited liability:** the owner must pay any debts the business's assets don't cover.
2. **Limited liability:** shareholders can lose only what they paid for their shares.
3. **Partnership profit:** add the ratio's numbers to get the parts; profit ÷ parts × the partner's parts.
4. **Dividend per share:** profit × payout % ÷ number of shares.

**Expert.** Match the needs: several owners rules out a sole trader; limited liability means a company; selling shares to the general public means a public limited company.

## Stakeholders

| Group | Type | Main interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employees | Internal | Fair pay and job security |
| Managers | Internal | Good salaries and the firm's growth |
| Shareholders | Internal | Profit and dividends |
| Customers | External | Good quality at a fair price |
| Suppliers | External | Regular orders and prompt payment |
| Government | External | Taxes paid and laws followed |
| Local community | External | Local jobs and little pollution |
| Lenders | External | Loans repaid on time with interest |

To find who gains and who loses: ask who gets more money or a better deal from the decision, and whose main interest it works against.

## SMART objectives

1. **Specific:** says exactly what will change.
2. **Measurable:** has a number to check against.
3. **Achievable:** realistic. Use the question's rule (here: no more than double last year's growth). With no number, you can't tell.
4. **Relevant:** fits the business's aims.
5. **Time-bound:** has a deadline.

Target = current figure × (1 + target % ÷ 100). The objective is met if the figure at the deadline reaches the target.

## SWOT analysis

1. Is the factor inside the firm (internal) or outside it (external)?
2. Does it help or harm the firm?

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal** | Strength | Weakness |
| **External** | Opportunity | Threat |

**Watch out:** a rival, a recession or a new law is external, so it can only be an opportunity or a threat.

## PESTLE analysis

1. **Political:** government decisions and stability (elections, spending plans, unrest).
2. **Economic:** the wider economy (interest rates, inflation, exchange rates, unemployment, recession).
3. **Social:** people and lifestyles (ageing population, trends, attitudes).
4. **Technological:** new technology (automation, apps, faster internet).
5. **Legal:** laws and regulations firms must obey.
6. **Environmental:** nature, weather and climate (floods, droughts, scarce resources).

If it is already a law, it is Legal; a government choice that isn't a law is Political.

**Expert.** A PESTLE factor is external, so in a firm's SWOT it is an opportunity if it helps that firm and a threat if it harms it. The same change can be both, for different firms.
