# Rhythm

How long notes last, how bars are organised, and how tempo turns beats into seconds. Everything is described in words, with no pictures or audio.

## Note values

Unless a question says otherwise, a **quarter note is one beat** (as in 4/4).

| Note | Beats | Dotted | Beats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| whole | 4 | dotted whole | 6 |
| half | 2 | dotted half | 3 |
| quarter | 1 | dotted quarter | 1.5 |
| eighth | 0.5 | dotted eighth | 0.75 |
| sixteenth | 0.25 | | |

A **dot** makes a note one and a half times as long. In British English these are the semibreve, minim, crotchet, quaver and semiquaver.

## Time signatures

- The **bottom number** names a note value (4 = quarter, 8 = eighth, 2 = half); the **top number** says how many of them fill a bar.
- **Simple time** (2/4, 3/4, 4/4…): each beat splits into two.
- **Compound time** (6/8, 9/8, 12/8): the notes are grouped in threes, and each group is one main beat. 6/8 has 2 main beats.
- Duple, triple and quadruple mean 2, 3 and 4 main beats per bar.

## Tempo

**BPM** means beats per minute. One beat lasts `60 ÷ BPM` seconds, and a piece lasts `beats × 60 ÷ BPM` seconds.

## Answer formats

- Type beats as decimals: `1.5`, `0.75`.
- Tempo answers that don't come out exactly say "round to 2 decimal places" (answers within 0.005 count).
- Tempo and bar-counting questions put their numbers in the calculator for you, as `_bpm`, `_bars` and so on.
