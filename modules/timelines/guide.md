# Timelines: how to solve

## General

1. Write each date on a number line in your head, with BC dates to the left of AD 1.
2. A bigger BC number is earlier: 490 BC came before 44 BC.
3. When a gap crosses from BC into AD, add the two numbers and take away 1, because there is no year 0.
4. Check the size makes sense: two events in the same century are less than 100 years apart.

Example: 31 BC to AD 14 is `31 + 14 - 1 = 44` years.

**Watch out:** BC years count down, so never just subtract a BC number from an AD one.

## Years between

1. Both AD: later year − earlier year.
2. Both BC: bigger BC number − smaller BC number.
3. BC to AD: BC number + AD number − 1.
4. An age works the same way, from the birth year to the year of the birthday.

Example: 1066 to 1492 is `1492 - 1066 = 426` years.

**Watch out:** adding a BC and an AD number counts a year 0 that never existed.

## Centuries & decades

1. If the year ends in 00, the century is the hundreds part (1900 is the 19th century).
2. Otherwise it is the hundreds part plus 1 (1450 is the 15th century).
3. BC centuries use the same rule on the BC number (490 BC is the 5th century BC).
4. The decade is the year with its last digit made 0 (1969 is in the 1960s).

Example: 1801 is the first year of the 19th century.

**Watch out:** the 1800s are the 19th century, not the 18th.

## Ordering

1. Turn any date given as "so many years after or before" into an actual year first.
2. Put every BC date before every AD date.
3. Among BC dates, bigger numbers go first; among AD dates, smaller numbers go first.
4. Read off the letters, in the direction the question asks.

Example: 490 BC, 44 BC, AD 79, 1066 are already earliest to latest.

**Watch out:** check whether the question wants earliest to latest or latest to earliest.

## Before, after & spans

1. Years ago = current year − event year (use the current year the question gives).
2. For a BC event, years ago = BC number + current year − 1.
3. "N years later" means add N; crossing from BC into AD, add 1 more to skip year 0.
4. An anniversary is that many years after the event.

Example: 30 years after 30 BC is AD 1, so 50 years after 30 BC is 20 more: AD 21.

**Watch out:** 50 years after 30 BC is not AD 20.

## Famous dates (recall)

1. Place each event in its era: ancient (BC), Roman and medieval, early modern (1500s to 1700s), or modern.
2. Within an era, think of the century, then the decade.
3. Use anchors you know well (1066, 1492, 1776, 1969) to compare.

Example: the Battle of Hastings (1066) came before Magna Carta (1215).

**Watch out:** two events with similar names can be centuries apart, such as the end of the First and of the Second World War.
