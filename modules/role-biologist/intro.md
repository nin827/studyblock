# Applied scenarios (Biologist)

Expert+ questions for the **Biologist** role. Each one is a single scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: probability, statistics, sampling, energy transfer, microscopy, dilutions or logarithms. A later part often builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Every part says how to round. If you reuse a rounded earlier answer, the later answer still comes out the same. Rules and formulas (Hardy–Weinberg, Lincoln–Petersen, the chi-square cut-off, Simpson's index, the energy passed between levels) are always stated in the question.

## Crosses and counts

A genetic cross gives an expected ratio, an expected count and a judgement on whether the observed counts are consistent with it, by a stated rule. At Expert+ you decide which of two crosses fits the data better, using chi-square.

## Gene pools

From the frequency of a recessive trait, find the allele frequency, the carriers and the alleles in the gene pool. At Expert+ you work backwards from a carrier count to the allele frequency.

## Field survey and energy

A capture-recapture estimate gives a population, a density and the energy available to the next level. At Expert+ you work backwards from a predator population to the producers and area it needs.

## Growth, cells and diversity

Doubling times, magnification and Simpson's diversity index. At Expert+ you work backwards from two counts to the doubling time, or find a missing species count and judge a site's diversity.

## Dilutions and colony counts

Lab maths for a microbiologist: serial dilution factors, colony-forming units (CFU) per mL from a plate count, picking the countable plate from a stated range, growth by doubling and making up a solution with C₁V₁ = C₂V₂. Large counts are typed as A in A × 10ⁿ, with n given. At Expert+ you find the generation time from two plate counts and judge whether a culture is ready in time, or plan which tube to plate later on.
