# How to solve: Applied scenarios (Biologist)

## General

- Read the whole scenario first, then work the parts in order: later parts usually reuse earlier results.
- Write the stated rules and constants into calculator rows (cut-offs, percentages passed on, areas).
- Keep full precision in the calculator and round only when you type each answer. Using an earlier rounded answer still gives the same later answer.
- For a yes or no judgement, compare your computed number with the stated limit.

## Crosses and counts

1. A cross of two heterozygotes (Aa × Aa) gives 3 dominant : 1 recessive, so the probabilities are 0.75 and 0.25.
2. A dihybrid cross of AaBb × AaBb gives 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, in sixteenths.
3. Expected count = probability × number of offspring.
4. Chi-square = the sum of (observed − expected)² ÷ expected over every class. Compare it with the stated cut-off.
5. For two possible crosses, work out chi-square for each. The smaller value is the better fit.

## Gene pools

1. Hardy–Weinberg: p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1. The frequency of the recessive trait is q².
2. So q = √(q²) and p = 1 − q. Carriers have frequency 2pq.
3. Counts = frequency × population size. Each individual carries 2 alleles.
4. Working backwards from the carrier frequency x: solve 2q(1 − q) = x with q below 0.5, so q = (1 − √(1 − 2x)) ÷ 2.

## Field survey and energy

1. Lincoln–Petersen: population = marked × second catch ÷ recaptured.
2. Density = population ÷ area.
3. Energy passed to the next level = energy held × the stated percentage.
4. Over several transfers, multiply by the percentage once for each transfer. Working backwards, divide by it.
5. Area = energy needed ÷ energy produced per unit area. Keep an eye on m² and hectares (1 ha = 10 000 m²).

## Growth, cells and diversity

1. Number of doublings = time ÷ doubling time. Population = start × 2^doublings.
2. To find the time to reach a target, use logs: time = doubling time × log₂(target ÷ start).
3. Actual size = image size ÷ magnification. Keep µm and mm straight (1 mm = 1000 µm).
4. Simpson's index D = 1 − Σ n(n − 1) ÷ (N(N − 1)), where n is the count of each species and N the total. A higher D means more diversity.
