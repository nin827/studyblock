# World History: how to solve

## General
- Build a mental timeline with a few anchor dates, then place other events before or after them.
- Link each event to a place and a person; one clue often brings back the other two.
- For year questions, type the year only. Watch BC vs AD: BC years count down towards AD 1.

## Ancient world
1. Place the civilisation on the map: Egypt on the Nile, Mesopotamia between the Tigris and Euphrates, Greece around the Aegean, Rome in Italy.
2. Match the achievement to the people: pyramids → Egypt, cuneiform → Sumerians, early democracy → Athens, roads and walls → Rome.
3. Rome's arc: republic → Julius Caesar killed (44 BC) → Augustus, first emperor → the West falls in AD 476.
4. Rome's rivals and neighbours: Carthage (Hannibal), Macedon (Alexander the Great).

Watch out: Julius Caesar was never emperor; his heir Augustus was the first.

## Medieval and early modern
1. Anchor dates: 1066 Hastings, 1215 Magna Carta, 1300s Black Death, 1453 fall of Constantinople, 1492 Columbus, 1517 Luther's theses.
2. Convert years to centuries by adding one to the leading digits: the 1300s are the **14th** century.
3. Name the war by its length: the Hundred Years' War (England vs France, Joan of Arc) and the Thirty Years' War (ended 1648).
4. Explorers: Columbus sailed west to the Americas; Vasco da Gama sailed around Africa to India.
5. Empires: Genghis Khan founded the Mongols; the Ottomans took Constantinople; the Mughals built the Taj Mahal.

Watch out: the Hundred Years' War actually lasted about 116 years, so don't do the arithmetic from the name.

## Revolutions and the 19th century
1. Anchor the revolutions: American independence 1776, French Revolution 1789, Napoleon's final defeat 1815.
2. Then the mid-century: Crimean War 1850s, US Civil War from 1861 (Lincoln), Meiji Restoration 1868, German unification 1871.
3. The Industrial Revolution began in Great Britain, then spread.
4. Link ideas to people: Marx and Engels wrote *The Communist Manifesto*.

Watch out: the Declaration of Independence (1776) and the French Revolution (1789) are close; America came first.

## 20th century
1. Anchor the wars: First World War 1914–1918 (Treaty of Versailles 1919), Russian Revolution 1917, Second World War 1939–1945.
2. Cold War milestones: Sputnik (Soviet Union, 1957), Moon landing (1969), fall of the Berlin Wall (1989).
3. Independence and rights: Gandhi's nonviolent movement in India; Mandela becomes South Africa's president in 1994.
4. For "which came first" questions, put each option on your timeline and pick the earliest.

Watch out: the USA joined the Second World War in 1941, two years after it began in Europe.
