## General

Economics problems chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (index points, percent, dollars, units).

1. Read the stated rules: how real figures are defined, the midpoint method, the rule of 70, the pricing rule.
2. A percentage change is change ÷ starting value (or ÷ average for the midpoint method).
3. Round only as each part asks. Work from the unrounded values, and the chained answers here stay the same.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Inflation and real wages

Inflation % = (new index − old index) ÷ old index × 100.

Real wage = nominal wage ÷ index × 100, so it is the wage in base-year prices. Real wage change % = (real₁ − real₀) ÷ real₀ × 100. Workers are better off if the real wage rose.

Backwards: new index = old index × (1 + nominal rise) ÷ (1 + real change).

## Elasticity and revenue

Midpoint method: % change = change ÷ average of the two values. Elasticity = |% change in quantity ÷ % change in price|. Above 1 is elastic, below 1 inelastic.

Revenue = price × quantity. If demand is inelastic, a price rise lifts revenue; if elastic, a price cut does.

Backwards: with a given elasticity E, the new quantity satisfies (Q₂ − Q₁) ÷ average Q = −E × (P₂ − P₁) ÷ average P.

## Taxes and who pays

Equilibrium: set quantity demanded equal to quantity supplied. A tax on sellers of $t shifts supply: buyers pay P, sellers keep P − t. Solve again for P.

Tax revenue = t × new quantity. Buyers' share of the tax = (rise in price buyers pay) ÷ t. The side with the less responsive curve bears more.

Backwards: change in quantity ÷ demand slope = rise in the price buyers pay.

## Growth and doubling time

Size after t years = start × (1 + rate)^t. GDP per person = GDP ÷ population. Per-person growth ≈ (1 + g) ÷ (1 + n) − 1.

Rule of 70: doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ rate in %. Exactly it is ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + rate).

Backwards: rate = (final ÷ start)^(1/t) − 1.
