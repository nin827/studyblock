# Electricity

Practise the core formulas of electric circuits. Each question gives you some quantities and asks for one missing value. Voltage is in volts (V), current in amps (A), resistance in ohms (Ω), power in watts (W) and energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `V = I × R` | Ohm's law: voltage = current × resistance |
| `P = V × I` | power = voltage × current |
| `P = I² × R` | power from current and resistance |
| `P = V² / R` | power from voltage and resistance |
| `E = P × t` | energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h) |
| `R = R1 + R2 + …` | resistors in series |
| `R = (R1 × R2) / (R1 + R2)` | two resistors in parallel |

Useful conversions: 1 kΩ = 1000 Ω, 1 mA = 0.001 A, 1 kW = 1000 W. Energy cost = energy in kWh × price per kWh.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Small whole numbers, series circuits, whole-number answers |
| Medium | mA and kΩ, P = V²/R, resistors in parallel (two, three, n identical, or the missing one), still whole-number answers |
| Hard | Decimal values, energy and cost in kWh, combined circuits, answers rounded to 2 decimal places |
| Expert | Multi-step circuit problems in two or three parts, each rounded as stated |

> [!TIP]
> On hard questions, round to 2 decimal places (answers within 0.005 are accepted). Two resistors in parallel always have less resistance than either one alone.

**Expert** questions combine ideas: a source with internal resistance (current, terminal voltage, lost volts), designing a potential divider, finding R from a change in voltage and current, choosing a fuse (3 A, 5 A or 13 A) and the running cost, a kettle heating water (Q = mcΔT, with c = 4200 J/(kg °C)), power lost in a transmission cable, the current in one branch of a series-parallel network, the resistor to add in parallel for a target total, and series versus parallel power. Currents may be asked to 3 decimal places. Use unrounded values from earlier parts.
