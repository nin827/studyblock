## General

These are navigation scenarios that chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (kt, nm, L, minutes, degrees).

1. Read the stated rules first: formulas, reserve minutes, contingency, offsets, the clearance or closing time.
2. Convert before you calculate: time in hours = minutes ÷ 60; distance ÷ speed gives hours, × 60 for minutes.
3. Round only as each part asks. The chained answers here do not change if you use an earlier rounded answer.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated limit.

## Wind triangle and ground speed

A headwind (wind from ahead) subtracts from the airspeed and a tailwind adds. Let θ be the angle between the track and the wind direction: the wind's part along the track is W × cos(θ) (a headwind if θ is under 90°) and across it W × sin(θ).

The wind correction angle comes from sin(WCA) = crosswind ÷ TAS. Point into the wind: a wind from the right of the track means a heading to the right of the track (track + WCA), and from the left means track − WCA.

Ground speed = TAS × cos(WCA) − headwind part. Time in minutes = distance ÷ ground speed × 60.

Working backwards: the ground speed needed is distance ÷ time. Then TAS² = (GS + headwind)² + crosswind².

## Fuel planning

Trip fuel = burn rate × time in hours. A reserve of R minutes at the burn rate is burn × R ÷ 60. Contingency of 10% is 10% of the trip fuel only. The total at departure adds trip fuel, contingency, reserve and taxi fuel.

Endurance in minutes = fuel ÷ burn × 60, less the reserve minutes. The furthest distance out from which the aircraft can still return (the point of no return) balances out and back: with ground speeds g₁ out and g₂ home it is endurance in hours × g₁ × g₂ ÷ (g₁ + g₂). The time to fly out is that distance ÷ g₁.

## Chart distances and bearings

Real distance = chart distance × scale. Convert centimetres to metres (÷ 100) and divide by 1852 for nautical miles.

The reciprocal of a bearing is the bearing ± 180° (kept between 0° and 360°).

For two legs, each leg's north part is length × cos(track) and its east part is length × sin(track). Add the north parts and the east parts; the distance back is the hypotenuse, and the bearing back points opposite to the movement, so find the quadrant first.

The distance from a point to a straight track is the length of the perpendicular: |e_P × n_B − n_P × e_B| ÷ AB, or AP × sin of the angle between AP and AB.

## ETA, time zones and descent

Work through UTC. Local time = UTC + offset, so UTC = local time − offset. Add the flight time (hours and minutes) to a UTC time, carrying minutes into hours.

Descent: height to lose = cruise altitude − target altitude. Time = height ÷ rate of descent. Distance = ground speed × time in hours. The aircraft is still before the top of descent if its distance to the target is more than that distance.
