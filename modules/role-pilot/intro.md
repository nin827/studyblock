# Applied scenarios (Pilot)

Expert+ questions for the **Pilot** role. Each one is a single scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: trigonometry for the wind triangle, fuel arithmetic, chart scales and bearings, clock and time-zone arithmetic, or rates for a descent. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

This role practises the arithmetic only: every figure is made up and every rule is stated in the question, so none of it is a procedure for real flying. Speeds are in knots (kt), distances in nautical miles (nm, 1 nm = 1852 m), fuel in litres, directions are true bearings in degrees, and a wind direction is where the wind blows from. Times use the 24-hour clock with fixed UTC offsets and no daylight saving. Every part says how to round.

## Wind triangle and ground speed

A headwind or tailwind gives a ground speed and a time. With a crosswind you find the wind correction angle with the sine rule, the heading, the ground speed and the time. At Expert+ you work backwards from a required arrival time to the airspeed needed, then judge whether the aircraft can do it.

## Fuel planning

Distance and ground speed give a flight time, then trip fuel, then the total needed with a stated reserve, taxi fuel and contingency, and a check against the fuel on board. At Expert+ you find the endurance and the point of no return.

## Chart distances and bearings

A distance measured on a chart is converted with the scale to nautical miles and a time, with the reciprocal track for the way back. At Expert and above you add two legs into a position and a direct bearing home, or find how far a danger area lies from a track.

## ETA, time zones and descent

A departure time and flight time give the arrival in UTC and in local time, and at Expert you check the arrival against a stated airport closing time. At Expert+ you work out the top of descent from the altitude to lose and the rate of descent, and judge whether the aircraft is still before it.
