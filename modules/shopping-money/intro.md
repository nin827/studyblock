# Shopping and Money

Money maths for everyday life: finding the best buy, working out sale prices and tax, splitting bills, budgeting your pay, and changing money for a trip. Prices are in dollars ($) and cents.

| Idea | How |
| --- | --- |
| Unit price | price ÷ amount (per item, per 100 g, per kg) |
| p% off | pay (100 − p)% of the price |
| t% tax | pay (100 + t)% of the price |
| Price before p% off | sale price ÷ (1 − p/100) |
| Two discounts | multiply what's left: (1 − a)(1 − b), not a + b |
| Dollars to another currency | × the rate |
| Back to dollars | ÷ the rate |

Exchange rates in the questions are made up, so always use the one given. Type money as a plain number, for example `12.5` or `12.50` for $12.50. Each question says how to round, usually to the nearest cent; round only at the end unless a part says to use an earlier rounded answer.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Whole prices, simple percentages, change from a note |
| Medium | Prices per 100 g, any percentage, sales tax, splitting a bill with a tip |
| Hard | Prices per kg, multi-buy deals, ordering best buys, two discounts, fees on exchange |
| Expert | Coupons that change the best buy, comparing stores, working back from a receipt, payslips, a trip's money |
