# Shopping and Money: how to solve

## General

- Work in one unit: all dollars (or all cents), all grams or all kilograms.
- Round only at the end, to the precision the question asks for (usually the nearest cent).
- Type money as a plain number: `12.5` and `12.50` both mean $12.50.

## Unit prices and best buys

1. Unit price = price ÷ amount. Choose the unit to compare: per item, per 100 g, per kg.
2. Put sizes in the same unit first: 750 g = 0.75 kg, 1.5 L = 1500 mL.
3. The lowest unit price is the best value.
4. "Buy 2, get 1 free": in every 3 you pay for 2. "3 for $5": use the deal as often as you can, then pay singles for the rest.
5. With a coupon, take it off the pack price before dividing.

Example: 750 g for $4.20 is `4.20 / 0.75 = 5.60` dollars per kg.

**Watch out:** a bigger pack is not always cheaper per gram.

## Discounts and tax

1. p% off: multiply by (1 − p/100). 25% off is × 0.75.
2. t% tax: multiply by (1 + t/100). 8% tax is × 1.08.
3. Discount then tax: multiply by both, and round at the end.
4. Two discounts in a row: multiply what is left after each; the total discount is 1 minus that.
5. Working backwards from a sale price, divide by (1 − p/100); from a price with tax, divide by (1 + t/100).

Example: 20% off then a further 10% off leaves `0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72`, so the total discount is 28%, not 30%.

**Watch out:** adding p% back to a sale price does not give the original price.

## Bills, pay and budgets

1. Change = note − total of the items.
2. Splitting a bill: add the tip first (× 1.15 for 15%), then divide by the number of people.
3. Pay = hours × rate; half an hour is 0.5. Overtime at 1.5 times means rate × 1.5 for those hours.
4. Budgets: turn each percentage into dollars, then subtract everything from the income.
5. Saving up: (goal − savings) ÷ weekly saving, rounded up to whole weeks.
6. Yearly costs: weekly × 52, monthly × 12.

Example: 3 people share $86 with a 15% tip: `86 × 1.15 / 3 ≈ 32.967`, so $32.97 each.

**Watch out:** tax is worked out on the gross pay, and take-home pay is gross minus tax.

## Currency exchange

1. "$1 = r units": dollars to the other currency, multiply by r; back to dollars, divide by r.
2. A fee or commission comes off the dollars before changing them.
3. To compare bureaux, work out how much you'd get from each.
4. To compare prices at home and abroad, change the foreign price into dollars.

Example: at $1 = 18.5 pesos, 740 pesos is `740 / 18.5 = 40` dollars.

**Watch out:** check the size of the answer. Changing dollars into a currency with a rate above 1 should give a bigger number.
