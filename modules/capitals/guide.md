# Capitals: how to solve

## General
- The capital is the seat of government, which is **not always the biggest or most famous city** (Canberra, not Sydney; Ottawa, not Toronto).
- Many capitals were chosen as a compromise between rival cities, or moved inland to a planned new city.
- Typed answers ignore capitals and extra spaces, but spelling counts. Accents are part of the name (Brasília, Bogotá).
- On a choice question, rule out the famous port or business city first, then pick between the rest.

## Europe
1. Start with the big ones you know, and use them as anchors for their neighbours.
2. Watch the "biggest city" trap: Switzerland's capital is **Bern**, not Zurich or Geneva (the Swiss picked a central, smaller city).
3. The Netherlands is a special case: the capital is Amsterdam, but the government sits in The Hague.
4. For small or Balkan states, attach a hook to the name: Montenegro's Podgorica means "under the little hill"; Liechtenstein's Vaduz is a short four-letter town, matching the tiny country.
5. Practise tricky spellings letter by letter, including accents and marks like the ș in Chișinău.

Watch out: Slovenia (Ljubljana) and Slovakia (Bratislava) are easy to swap.

## Asia
1. Again, the biggest city is often not the capital: Turkey moved its capital from Istanbul to **Ankara**, and Vietnam's is in the north, not Ho Chi Minh City in the south.
2. Some capitals were newly built: Myanmar moved its government from Yangon to a purpose-built city in 2005.
3. India's capital is **New Delhi**, the planned district inside Delhi.
4. Sri Lanka has two: Colombo is the commercial capital; the legislative one is the long name, Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte.
5. For small mountain or river countries, picture one landmark: Laos's capital sits on the Mekong; Bhutan's in a Himalayan valley.

Watch out: Shanghai, Mumbai and Istanbul are huge, but none of them is a capital.

## Americas
1. **USA**: Washington, D.C., not New York. **Canada**: Ottawa, chosen between rivals Toronto and Montreal.
2. **Brazil** moved its capital inland in 1960 to the planned city of **Brasília**, so it is not Rio or São Paulo.
3. Spanish names often mean something: Asunción is "Assumption", Santiago is "Saint James", and Bogotá and Quito sit high in the Andes.
4. Belize moved its capital inland to the planned town of Belmopan after a hurricane flooded Belize City.
5. For the long or unusual ones (Tegucigalpa, Paramaribo), say them out loud in syllables: Te-gu-ci-gal-pa.

Watch out: Mexico's capital shares the country's name, Mexico City; don't overthink it.
