# Health Statistics

For learning only. This isn't medical advice; for anything about your own health, ask a qualified professional.

The arithmetic behind population health figures: how common something is, how well a screening test sorts people, how two groups' risks compare, and how to summarise a set of measurements. Every question is about groups of people, and defines the measure it asks for.

| Measure | How to find it |
| --- | --- |
| Prevalence | people with the condition ÷ population (× 100 for a percentage) |
| Incidence | new cases in a year ÷ population × 1,000 (or 100,000) |
| Percentage change | (new - old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Sensitivity | TP ÷ (TP + FN): positives among those *with* the condition |
| Specificity | TN ÷ (TN + FP): negatives among those *without* it |
| PPV / NPV | TP ÷ all positives / TN ÷ all negatives |
| Risk | outcomes ÷ everyone in the group |
| Odds | outcomes ÷ people without the outcome |
| Relative risk / odds ratio | exposed group's risk (odds) ÷ unexposed group's risk (odds) |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Round numbers and whole-number answers |
| Medium | Real-looking counts, answers rounded to 1 or 2 decimal places |
| Hard | Rates across years, building a screening table from percentages, odds ratios, quartiles; some questions have several parts |

**How to type answers.** Percentages go in without the `%` sign; use a minus sign for a decrease. Percentages and rates are rounded to 1 decimal place (answers within 0.05 count), ratios and means to 2 decimal places (within 0.005). Each question says how to round.
