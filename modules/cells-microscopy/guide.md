# Cells & Microscopy: how to solve

## General

- Write down each value with its unit before you start, and put them in the calculator as rows (`_image`, `_actual`, …).
- Convert to the same unit first: 1 mm = 1000 µm, 1 µm = 1000 nm, 1 m = 1000 mm.
- Going to a smaller unit makes the number bigger (multiply); going to a bigger unit makes it smaller (divide).
- Round only at the end, as the question asks.

## Magnification

1. Use `M = image ÷ actual`, or rearrange: `image = M × actual`, `actual = image ÷ M`.
2. Convert the image size (usually mm) into the unit of the actual size (µm or nm).
3. With a scale bar, find M from the bar first (its image length ÷ the length on its label), then use it for the cell. Or scale by proportion: `actual = cell image ÷ bar image × bar label`.

Example: an image of 6 mm = 6000 µm of a cell 20 µm long gives `M = 6000 ÷ 20 = 300`.

**Watch out:** dividing mm by µm without converting gives an answer 1000 times too small.

## Units & sizes

1. Count the steps between the units: m → mm → µm → nm, each a factor of 1000 (cm → mm is 10).
2. Multiply when going to a smaller unit, divide when going to a bigger one.
3. Standard form: a number from 1 to just under 10, times a power of ten. 1 mm = 10⁻³ m, 1 µm = 10⁻⁶ m, 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.
4. To compare sizes, put both in the same unit and divide. "How many fit" means round **down**.

Example: 25 µm = 25 × 10⁻⁶ m = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m.

**Watch out:** moving the decimal point one place left in the front number adds 1 to the power.

## Cell division

1. Each generation doubles the number of cells: after n generations there are `start × 2ⁿ`.
2. Generations = time ÷ doubling time (convert hours to minutes first).
3. To reach a target, divide target by start and find the power of two; if it isn't exact, round the number of generations **up**.
4. Time = generations × doubling time.

Example: doubling every 20 min for 2 hours = 120 ÷ 20 = 6 generations, so 1 cell becomes 2⁶ = 64.

**Watch out:** don't multiply by 2 × n; doubling n times multiplies by 2ⁿ.

## Surface area to volume

1. Cube: surface area = 6 × side², volume = side³.
2. Cuboid: surface area = 2 × (lw + lh + wh), volume = l × w × h.
3. SA:V = surface area ÷ volume, written as one number (3 means 3 : 1).
4. A bigger SA:V means more membrane for each unit of volume, so faster exchange by diffusion.

Example: a 2 µm cube has SA 24 µm² and volume 8 µm³, so SA:V = 3.

**Watch out:** the bigger cell has the **smaller** ratio. Cutting a cube into smaller cubes adds surface without changing the volume.
