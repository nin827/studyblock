# Cells & Microscopy

The maths behind looking at cells: how much a microscope magnifies, how small cells really are, how fast they multiply, and why small cells exchange substances quickly.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `magnification = image size ÷ actual size` | both sizes in the same unit |
| `cells = start × 2ⁿ` | after n doublings, if every cell divides and none die |
| `generations = time ÷ doubling time` | how many doublings fit into the time |
| `SA:V = surface area ÷ volume` | bigger ratio = faster exchange for its size |

**Units:** 1 m = 1000 mm, 1 mm = 1000 µm (micrometres), 1 µm = 1000 nm (nanometres), and 1 cm = 10 mm.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | One-step conversions, same-unit magnification, cubes |
| Medium | Converting mm to µm, doubling times, cuboids |
| Hard | Electron micrographs in nm, scale bars, standard form, time to reach a target, comparing cells |

**Answer formats:** give magnifications as a plain number (`400` for ×400). Each question says how to round; answers within half of the last decimal place count. Standard-form sizes are picked from a list.
