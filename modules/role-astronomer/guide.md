# How to solve: Applied scenarios (Astronomer)

## General

- Read the whole scenario first, then work the parts in order: later parts usually reuse earlier results.
- Write the stated constants and rules into calculator rows (3.26 light-years per parsec, 116, 7 mm, 4 minutes).
- Keep full precision in the calculator and round only when you type each answer. Using an earlier rounded answer still gives the same later answer.
- For a yes or no or pick-one judgement, compare your computed number with the stated limit. "At or before" and "at least" include the limit.

## Parallax and star distances

1. Distance in parsecs = 1 ÷ parallax in arcseconds. Parallax and distance are inverse.
2. Light-years = parsecs × 3.26. A star that is d light-years away sent its light d years ago.
3. 1 arcsecond = 1000 milliarcseconds.
4. A parallax that is f times larger means a distance f times smaller.
5. To compare with a survey range, convert to the range's unit first.

## Magnitudes and brightness

1. A smaller magnitude means a brighter star.
2. Brightness ratio = 10^(0.4 × Δm). Five magnitudes is a factor of 100.
3. Distance modulus m − M = 5 × log₁₀(d ÷ 10), with d in parsecs. So M = m minus the modulus and m = M plus it.
4. Working back: d = 10 × 10^((m − M) ÷ 5). A star at distance d looks (d ÷ 10)² times fainter than at 10 parsecs.
5. For A × 10ⁿ, divide by the power of ten shown and round A.

## Telescope optics

1. Magnification = telescope focal length ÷ eyepiece focal length.
2. Light gathering compared with the eye = (aperture ÷ 7 mm)². For two telescopes it is (larger ÷ smaller)².
3. A telescope that gathers k times more light reaches 2.5 × log₁₀(k) magnitudes fainter.
4. Smallest separation split, in arcseconds = 116 ÷ aperture in mm. A pair can be split if its separation is at least that.
5. To pick an eyepiece, find the focal length you need, then take the one in the case with the smallest gap.

## Observing schedules

1. A star rises 4 minutes earlier each night. After n nights, subtract 4n minutes.
2. Nights needed = minutes to move earlier ÷ 4, rounded up.
3. For two stars, work each one, then take the larger count.
4. If a subtraction goes below 00:00, add 24 hours.
5. Nights between two sightings = the difference in minutes ÷ 4.
