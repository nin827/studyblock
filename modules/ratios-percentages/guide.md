# How to solve: Ratios and Percentages

## General

- Find the value of **one part** (or one unit, or 1%) first, then scale up. Almost every question here is that idea.
- Put both amounts in the **same unit** before you compare them.
- Give ratios in simplest whole numbers with a colon and no spaces, like `3:4`. Give percentages without the % sign, with a minus sign for a decrease.
- Keep full values in calculator rows (`_one`, `_m`, …) and round to 2 decimal places only at the end, when the question asks.

## Ratios

1. To simplify, divide every part by the greatest common factor. Clear decimals first (× 10) and convert units first.
2. To share an amount, add the parts to get the total number of parts.
3. One part = total ÷ number of parts.
4. Multiply one part by the parts you need. For `1:n`, divide both parts by the first part.
5. For similar shapes, if lengths scale by k, areas scale by k² and volumes by k³.

Example: share 60 in the ratio `2:3`. That's 5 parts, so one part is 12 and the larger share is 3 × 12 = 36.

**Watch out:** when two ratios share a letter (a:b and b:c), make the shared part equal before you combine them.

## Proportion

1. Decide the type. **Direct** means more of one gives more of the other; **inverse** means more of one gives less of the other.
2. Direct: find the amount for one (`_v1 / _n1`), then multiply by the new number.
3. Inverse: the product stays the same. Work out the total (`_n1 * _t1`), then divide by the new number.
4. For `y ∝ x²`, `y ∝ √x` or `y ∝ 1/x`: write `y = k × x²` (or `k / x`, …), find k from the known pair, then substitute.

Example: 4 pumps take 6 hours, so the job is 24 pump-hours. 3 pumps take 24 ÷ 3 = 8 hours.

**Watch out:** a rate is "per one": divide the amount by the number of units, not the other way round.

## Percentages

1. Turn the percentage into a **multiplier**: +15% is × 1.15, and -15% is × 0.85.
2. Percent of an amount: `p / 100 × amount`. One amount as a percentage of another: `part / whole × 100`.
3. Percentage change: `(new - old) / old × 100`. Always divide by the old value.
4. Reverse percentage: divide the new value by the multiplier to get the original.
5. Several changes: multiply the multipliers. Repeated changes use a power, `start × m^n`.

Example: after a 20% increase a value is 300, so the original is 300 ÷ 1.2 = 250.

**Watch out:** never add successive percentages, and never take the percentage of the *new* value when you need the original.
