# Calculus Basics

Calculus is the maths of change. **Differentiating** a function gives its gradient (how steep it is) at every point; **integrating** goes the other way and gives the area under a curve. Here you practise both on polynomials, plus a few powers like `√x` and `1/x²`.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Derivatives | Use the power rule to find `f'(x)`, then values like `f'(2)` or `f''(1)` |
| Gradients & tangents | The gradient at a point, where the gradient takes a value, and tangent lines `y = mx + c` |
| Stationary points | Where the gradient is zero, and whether it is a maximum or a minimum |
| Integration | Indefinite integrals (`+ C`), definite integrals and areas |

**Answer formats**

- Expressions (a derivative, an integral, a tangent line) are **multiple choice**. Powers are written like `x³`, `x⁻²` (that is `1/x²`) and `x^(1/2)` (that is `√x`).
- Easy and medium numbers are **whole numbers**. Hard answers say "Round to 2 decimal places"; answers within 0.005 count.
- The calculator has no derivative button, so differentiate by hand, then use it to substitute. Write powers with `^`, like `3*_x^2` or `_x^(1/2)`.
