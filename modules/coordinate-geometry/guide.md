# Coordinate Geometry: how to solve

## General

- Label the points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) before you substitute, and put negative numbers in brackets.
- Take differences in the same order: always "second point minus first point" for both x and y.
- Keep gradients and intercepts as fractions; simplify only at the end.
- The calculator starts with the coordinates as `_x1`, `_y1`, `_x2`, `_y2`.

## Points & distance

1. Midpoint: average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates.
2. Distance: find the change in x and the change in y, square both, add, and take the square root.
3. For an exact answer, simplify the root: take out the largest square factor, so √45 = √(9 × 5) = 3√5.
4. For a right angle in a triangle, compare squared side lengths: the biggest equals the sum of the other two exactly when the angle opposite it is 90°.

Example: from (1, 2) to (4, 6) the steps are 3 and 4, so the distance is √(9 + 16) = 5, and the midpoint is (2.5, 4).

**Watch out:** square each step before adding. √(3 + 4) is not the distance.

## Gradient & lines

1. Gradient m = (y2 − y1) ÷ (x2 − x1). Simplify the fraction.
2. To read m and c from an equation, rearrange it to y = mx + c.
3. For the line through two points, find m, then put one point into y = mx + c to find c.
4. To clear fractions, multiply every term by the denominator and write the line as ax + by = c.

Example: through (1, 3) and (3, 7), m = 4 ÷ 2 = 2; then 3 = 2 × 1 + c gives c = 1, so y = 2x + 1.

**Watch out:** keep the subtraction order the same on top and bottom, or the sign of the gradient flips.

## Parallel & perpendicular

1. Rearrange each line to y = mx + c to find its gradient.
2. Parallel lines have equal gradients. Equal gradients and equal intercepts mean the same line.
3. Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1: flip the fraction and change its sign, so 2/3 becomes -3/2 (type a minus sign as `-`).
4. For a line through a given point, use the new gradient and the point in y = mx + c to find c.

Example: perpendicular to y = 2x + 5 through (4, 1): m = -1/2, and 1 = -1/2 × 4 + c gives c = 3, so `y = -(1/2)x + 3`.

**Watch out:** a perpendicular bisector must pass through the midpoint, not through one of the end points.

## Intersections

1. On the x-axis y = 0; on the y-axis x = 0. Substitute and solve.
2. Where two lines meet, both equations are true: substitute one into the other, solve for x, then find y.
3. For a line and a circle, substitute the line into x² + y² = r², or test each option in both equations.
4. Triangle area from corners: Area = |x1(y2 − y3) + x2(y3 − y1) + x3(y1 − y2)| ÷ 2.

Example: y = 2x − 1 and y = −x + 5 meet where 2x − 1 = −x + 5, so x = 2 and y = 3.

**Watch out:** check your meeting point in both equations, and remember to halve in the area formula.
