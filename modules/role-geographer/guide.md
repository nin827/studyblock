## General

Geography problems chain simple measurements, so work in order and keep the units straight (cm, m, km, minutes, mm, °C).

1. Read the stated rules first: map scale, grid-reference format, steepness rule, climate bands.
2. Convert units before you calculate (100 000 cm = 1 km; 100 cm = 1 m).
3. Round only as each part asks. The chained answers here are exact, so a rounded earlier answer gives the same later answer.
4. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Map scale and walking time

Real distance = map length × scale number, then convert cm to km (÷ 100 000).

Walking time (minutes) = distance ÷ speed × 60. Return time = start + walk out + stay + walk back.

To work backwards, start from the sunset limit and take off the margin, the walking time and the stay.

## Grid references and routes

Split each 6-figure reference into easting and northing (tenths of a km). The differences are the two short sides of a right-angled triangle.

Distance = √(Δeasting² + Δnorthing²). Bearing = tan⁻¹(Δeasting ÷ Δnorthing) measured clockwise from north, adjusted for the quadrant (add 180° if the finish is south of the start, and 360° if the result would be negative).

A feature lies on the straight line if its easting and northing fit between the two ends in the same proportion; the halfway point averages each pair.

A dog-leg goes along one axis, then the other: its length is Δeasting + Δnorthing.

## Contours and gradients

Climb = contour intervals crossed × interval, or the difference of two spot heights.

Gradient 1 in N means N metres along the ground for each 1 metre climbed: N = ground distance ÷ climb. A smaller N is steeper.

Ground distance = map length × scale number, then convert to metres. The slope angle satisfies tan(angle) = 1 ÷ N.

## Climate tables and biomes

Range = highest monthly temperature − lowest. Total rainfall = sum of the twelve months. Mean temperature = sum ÷ 12 (mind the minus signs).

The driest month has the smallest rainfall figure.

For the biome, find the temperature band and the rainfall band from the stated cut-offs, then look up the pair in the stated table. If one month is missing, subtract the eleven known months from the stated annual total.
