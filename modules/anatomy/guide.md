# Anatomy: how to solve

## General
- Link each part to its **job** or its **location**: most questions describe one and ask for the other.
- Everyday names have anatomical twins: kneecap = patella, collarbone = clavicle, shoulder blade = scapula, breastbone = sternum, shinbone = tibia.
- Check the requested form: one word, a number, or a choice. Spelling counts for typed answers.

## Skeleton
1. Place the bone: head, chest, arm, hip or leg.
2. Upper limb: humerus (upper arm), then radius and ulna (forearm; the radius is on the thumb side).
3. Lower limb: femur (thigh), then tibia and fibula (lower leg; the tibia is the shinbone).
4. Remember the record holders: 206 bones in an adult, longest is the femur, smallest is the stapes in the middle ear.

Example: "the bone between the shoulder and the elbow" is the humerus.

Watch out: the clavicle (collarbone) is at the front; the scapula (shoulder blade) is at the back.

## Organs
1. Decide which job is described: pumping, breathing, digesting, filtering or controlling.
2. Follow food in order: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (absorbs nutrients), large intestine (absorbs water).
3. Helpers: the liver makes bile, the gallbladder stores it, the pancreas makes enzymes and hormones.
4. Kidneys filter blood into urine; the ureters carry it to the bladder.

Example: "where most food is absorbed" is the small intestine.

Watch out: the skin is the largest organ overall, but the liver is the largest organ inside the body.

## Body systems
1. Ask what the part does, then name the system that does that job (see the table below).
2. Glands that release hormones into the blood belong to the endocrine system.
3. Airways (trachea, bronchi) are respiratory; food tubes (oesophagus, intestines) are digestive.

Example: the thyroid gland releases hormones, so it is in the endocrine system.

| System | Main parts | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circulatory | heart, blood, blood vessels | carries oxygen and nutrients |
| Respiratory | trachea, bronchi, lungs | gas exchange |
| Digestive | stomach, intestines, liver | breaks down and absorbs food |
| Nervous | brain, spinal cord, nerves | carries signals |
| Skeletal | bones, joints | support and protection |
| Muscular | muscles | movement |
| Urinary | kidneys, ureters, bladder | removes waste as urine |
| Endocrine | thyroid, pituitary, other glands | hormones |
| Immune and lymphatic | lymph nodes, white blood cells | fights infection |
| Integumentary | skin, hair, nails | outer covering |

Watch out: sweat glands are in the skin, so they are integumentary, not endocrine.

## Blood and circulation
1. Blood parts: red cells carry oxygen, white cells fight infection, platelets help clotting, plasma carries everything.
2. Vessels: **A**rteries go **A**way from the heart, veins return to it, capillaries are where exchange happens.
3. Heart: two atria on top receive blood, two ventricles below pump it out; valves stop backflow.
4. Route: body, venae cavae, right atrium, right ventricle, pulmonary artery, lungs, pulmonary veins, left atrium, left ventricle, aorta, body.

Example: the right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.

Watch out: the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood, and the pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood.
