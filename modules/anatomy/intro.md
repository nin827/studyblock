# Anatomy

*For learning only. This isn't medical advice; for anything about your own health, ask a qualified professional.*

The basic map of the human body, as taught in introductory courses: the major bones, what the main organs do, which body system each part belongs to, and how blood travels around the body.

| Topic | Key facts |
| --- | --- |
| Skeleton | An adult has 206 bones; the femur (thigh) is the longest, the stapes (middle ear) the smallest |
| Organs | The liver makes bile; most food is absorbed in the small intestine; the large intestine absorbs water |
| Body systems | Circulatory, respiratory, digestive, nervous, skeletal, muscular, urinary, endocrine, immune and lymphatic, integumentary |
| Blood and circulation | Red cells carry oxygen, white cells fight infection, platelets help clotting; arteries go away from the heart, veins towards it |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Everyday names (kneecap, collarbone) and the best-known organs and systems |
| Medium | Anatomical names, digestion, heart chambers and vessels |
| Hard | Middle-ear bones, glands, pulmonary circulation and the route through the heart |

**Tip:** most questions are multiple choice. Typed answers ignore capital letters, but spelling counts; the question says when to answer in one word or a number.
