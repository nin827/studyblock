## General

Journalist scenarios chain simple steps, so work in order and keep the units straight (counts, %, percentage points, words, minutes).

1. Read the stated rules first: the margin-of-error rule, the fairness test, the page size, the time zones.
2. Write down which number each part asks for, and its rounding.
3. Use the rounded figure from an earlier part only where the question says to; otherwise work from the exact values.
4. For a yes or no, find the numbers first, then compare them using the stated rule. Equal to the limit counts as within it, unless the rule says "more than".

## Checking a claim

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100; a fall is negative. A rate per 100 000 = count ÷ population × 100 000. A rate takes the population into account, so a count can rise while the rate changes less (or even falls).

A change in **percentage points** is the difference of two percentages (45% − 38% = 7 points). The **relative** change is that difference divided by the old percentage (7 ÷ 38 × 100 ≈ 18.4%). They are different numbers: a headline saying "points" or "%" is only accurate if it uses the matching one.

## Polls and margins of error

Use the rule in the question: margin of error (points) = 100 ÷ √n. A lead is the gap between the two shares, in points. Compare it with the limit stated in the question (such as "more than twice the margin"). To find the poll size for a target margin T, rearrange: n = (100 ÷ T)², then round up to whole people.

For two polls, the change in a share is the later minus the earlier, with its sign. Test its size against the stated combined margin.

## Page space and deadlines

Space = columns × words per column − the words taken by headline, pictures and boxes. Words to cut = story length − space. Reading time = words ÷ words per minute. When the same number must be cut from each of two stories, split the total in two and round up.

Time zones: UTC+N means N hours ahead of UTC (half hours count). To move from one zone to another, add the difference of the offsets, newsroom offset minus local offset. To find the latest local time, subtract that difference from the newsroom deadline.

## Averages that mislead

Mean = total ÷ number of values. Median = the middle value once the list is in order (odd count). One very high value pulls the mean above the median, so the mean can describe hardly anyone. To count people below the mean, compare each value with the exact mean.

To test how far the mean overstates the typical pay: (mean − median) ÷ median × 100, then compare with the stated percentage.
