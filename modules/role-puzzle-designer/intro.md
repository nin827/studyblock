# Applied scenarios (Puzzle Designer)

Scenario questions for the **Puzzle Designer** role, from Medium to Expert+. A designer has to check a puzzle, not just solve it: does it have exactly one answer, is it really magic, how many different designs exist, and was it too hard for the people who tried it. Each question is one made-up scenario with several linked answers, and each part says how to give its answer. Counts are whole numbers and exact.

## Grid puzzles

A small 4×4 Latin square (each digit once per row and column) with some clues. You count the digits a cell could hold, then how many ways the grid can be completed, which cells are forced, which extra clue makes the puzzle unique, and which clues a finished puzzle really needs.

## Magic squares

A 3×3 grid whose rows, columns and diagonals all add to the same sum. You find the sum and the missing entries, track down a single typo in a draft from the lines that fail, and at the top level count the ways to complete a 1 to 9 square that has a few clues.

## Counting designs

How many different codes a panel, padlock or ring of lamps allows under stated rules: symbols reused or not, no equal neighbours, a code and its reverse counted as one, or a ring that may be rotated and turned over. You then decide whether there are enough designs for the number of products needed.

## Playtest results

Solve times and give-up counts from testers. You find the median time and the percentage who solved it, work out a stated difficulty rating, rank two puzzles, and apply a stated rule to call a puzzle too easy, about right or too hard.
