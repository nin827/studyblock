## General

These are calculation drills with invented numbers. Read the constants the question states, work the parts in order, and round only as each part asks.

1. Note every stated constant (energy factors, reference intake, MET values, conversions).
2. Each part usually feeds the next.
3. A rounded earlier answer gives the same later answer.
4. For a Yes/No or choice part, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Label energy

Energy per 100 g = 4 × carbohydrate + 4 × protein + 9 × fat (kcal, grams per 100 g). Energy per portion = energy per 100 g × portion mass ÷ 100. Percentage of the reference intake = energy ÷ reference intake × 100.

Share of energy from fat = 9 × fat ÷ total energy × 100. To work backwards, find the energy per 100 g first, then subtract the carbohydrate and protein energy and divide by 9.

## Portions and recipes

Energy of an ingredient = grams × kcal per 100 g ÷ 100. Add the ingredients for the recipe total, then divide by the number of servings.

To scale a recipe, multiply every amount by new servings ÷ old servings.

On a packet, the portion mass is the per-portion energy ÷ the per-100 g energy × 100. Packets needed is a rounded-up count: part of a packet still means opening one more.

## Activity energy

Energy (kcal) = MET × body mass (kg) × time (hours). Convert minutes to hours first. kJ = kcal × 4.184. Energy per minute = the session's kcal ÷ its minutes.

To work backwards, rearrange: body mass = energy ÷ (MET × hours).

## Fluid volumes

With 1 kg of body mass = 1 L of fluid, the fluid lost is the mass change plus the fluid drunk. Rate = volume ÷ hours. Percentage of start mass = change ÷ start mass × 100.

Bottles needed is a rounded-up count: a part-filled bottle still counts as one.
