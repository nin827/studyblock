## General

Design problems chain simple measurements, so work in order and keep the units straight (mm, m, m², $).

1. Read the stated rules first: scale, comfort range, minimum glazing, steepest gradient, contingency, budget.
2. Convert units before you calculate (1 000 mm = 1 m; 1 m² = 1 000 000 mm²).
3. Round only as each part asks. The chained answers here are exact, so a rounded earlier answer gives the same later answer.
4. Counts of things you must have enough of (risers, flights, windows, slabs, trays) round **up**. How many fit within a limit rounds **down**.
5. For a verdict, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Plans at scale and cost

Real length = drawing length × scale number (1 : 50 means 1 mm on the plan is 50 mm in reality). Divide by 1 000 for metres.

Area = real length × real width. Cost = area × rate. A contingency of c% multiplies the cost by (100 + c) ÷ 100.

To work backwards, divide the budget by (100 + c) ÷ 100, then by the rate, to get the area; divide by the width for the length; divide by the scale number for the length on the plan.

## Stairs and the 2R + G rule

Number of risers = floor-to-floor height ÷ the largest allowed riser, **rounded up**. Riser height R = height ÷ number of risers.

Treads = risers − 1, so total going = treads × going G.

Comfort sum = 2R + G, compared with the stated range. Pitch angle: tan(pitch) = R ÷ G, then use the inverse tangent. When the run is fixed, G = run ÷ treads.

## Glazing and daylight

Window area = width × height; total = number of windows × one window. Glazing ratio = glazing area ÷ floor area × 100.

To find windows needed: required area ÷ one window, rounded **up**. To find the most windows allowed under a maximum: limit ÷ one window, rounded **down**.

For a wall: k windows need k × width + (k + 1) × the stated pier length.

## Ramps and landings

Run = rise × N (gradient 1 in N). Change the rise to metres first.

Flights = run ÷ longest flight, rounded **up**. Landings go between flights, so there is one fewer landing than flights. Total = run + landings × landing length.

For a strip of land, find the largest k with k × flight + (k − 1) × landing at most the strip length, then multiply k by the rise of one flight (flight length ÷ N).

## Planting and paving quantities

Planted area = garden area − patio area. Plants = planted area × plants per m². Trays = plants ÷ tray size, rounded **up**.

Slabs are laid in a grid: count the slabs along each side (side in mm ÷ slab size, rounded **up**, because a cut slab counts as a whole slab), then multiply the two counts. Dividing the patio area by one slab's area gives too few, because the offcuts are not reused.

When the paving is bought first, trays affordable = (budget − paving cost) ÷ price per tray, rounded **down**. Compare plants from those trays with the plants the bed needs, and with half of that.
