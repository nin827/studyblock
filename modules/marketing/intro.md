# Marketing

The standard textbook tools marketers use to plan and judge products. The firms and numbers are made up, so the questions are practice, not real market data.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing mix (4Ps) | Sort decisions into Product, Price, Place and Promotion |
| Ansoff matrix | Name a growth strategy from whether the product and the market are new, and compare risk |
| Boston matrix | Place products as Stars, Cash cows, Question marks or Dogs from market growth and relative share |
| Product life cycle | Name the stage from sales and profit, and the usual marketing focus there |
| Pricing strategies | Recognise skimming, penetration, premium and other strategies; work out cost-plus prices, markup and margin |
| Market segmentation | Sort criteria into demographic, geographic, psychographic and behavioural bases; size a segment |

**Answer formats**

- Many questions show options as blocks in columns, for example `Existing | New | Market development`. Compare the options column by column: most differ in only one or two places.
- Money and percentages: type the number only, no `$` or `%`. Each question says how to round.
- Each question states its rule, for example "high growth means above 10% a year", so you never need to guess a threshold.

**Levels.** Easy names one thing; Medium and Hard combine two or three facts, or add a calculation. **Expert** questions combine frameworks or need several steps: a three-product Boston portfolio, the life cycle and the Boston matrix together, and skimming versus penetration by total contribution.
