# Marketing: how to solve

## General

- Read the rule the question gives you (thresholds, stages) and apply it exactly.
- With block options, decide each column on its own, then find the option that matches all of them.
- Keep full precision in the calculator and round only at the end.

## Marketing mix (4Ps)

1. **Product:** the item itself: features, quality, design, packaging, brand name, range, warranty and after-sales service.
2. **Price:** what customers pay and how: price level, price changes, bulk prices, payment by instalments, pricing methods.
3. **Place:** how it reaches customers: shops, websites, wholesalers, vending machines, delivery and warehouses.
4. **Promotion:** how customers hear about it and are persuaded: adverts, influencers, sponsorship, free samples, newsletters, sales offers such as "buy one, get one free".

**Watch out:** a "buy one, get one free" offer is a sales promotion, so it is Promotion, even though it changes what people pay. A logo on a team's shirts is sponsorship, also Promotion.

## Ansoff matrix

1. Is the product existing or new to the firm?
2. Is the market (the customers) existing or new?
3. Read the matrix:

| | Existing market | New market |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Existing product** | Market penetration | Market development |
| **New product** | Product development | Diversification |

Risk grows with each new thing: market penetration is the safest, product and market development are in the middle, and diversification is the riskiest.

## Boston matrix

1. Market growth % = (this year − last year) ÷ last year × 100.
2. Relative market share = the product's sales (or share) ÷ the largest rival's. Above 1 means it leads its market.
3. Use the thresholds in the question (here: growth above 10%, relative share above 1).

| | High share | Low share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High growth** | Star | Question mark |
| **Low growth** | Cash cow | Dog |

Usual actions: build question marks, hold stars, harvest cash cows (use their cash elsewhere) and divest dogs.

**Watch out:** divide by last year's market size, not this year's, and put the product's own sales on top of the share ratio.

## Product life cycle

1. Sales change % = (this year − last year) ÷ last year × 100.
2. Apply the question's rule: rising sales with a loss is Introduction; with a profit, up 15% or more is Growth, within 5% is Maturity; down 5% or more is Decline.
3. Only the latest year decides the stage; earlier rows are history.

| Stage | Usual focus |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Informative advertising (make people aware) |
| Growth | Widen distribution |
| Maturity | Extension strategy (new uses, new markets, updated versions) |
| Decline | Cut support or withdraw |

## Pricing strategies

1. **Skimming:** high launch price for keen early buyers while there are no rivals, then lowered.
2. **Penetration:** low launch price to win market share fast, raised later.
3. **Premium:** always high, to signal quality and exclusivity.
4. **Cost-plus:** full cost per unit × (1 + markup ÷ 100); full cost per unit = variable cost + fixed costs ÷ units.
5. **Competitive:** close to rivals' prices. **Psychological:** just below a round number ($9.99). **Loss leader:** below cost to bring in shoppers. **Price discrimination:** different prices for the same thing to different groups or at different times.

Markup = profit per unit ÷ cost × 100; margin = profit per unit ÷ price × 100.

**Expert.** Contribution = (price − variable cost) × units. When fixed costs are the same in both plans, the plan with the bigger total contribution earns the bigger profit.

## Market segmentation

1. **Demographic:** who people are: age, gender, income, family, occupation.
2. **Geographic:** where they are: region, climate, town or country, distance.
3. **Psychographic:** how they live and think: lifestyle, values, personality, interests.
4. **Behavioural:** how they act towards the product: how often they buy, loyalty, occasion, benefits sought.

Segment size: multiply the population by each percentage in turn (the second percentage is a share of the first group).
