# Vocabulary: how to solve

## General
- Say the word in a sentence you know; context often unlocks meaning.
- Look for roots and prefixes: *bene-* is good, *mal-* is bad, *un-*/*in-* is not.
- Decide if the word is positive or negative, then cross out options with the wrong feeling.
- In sentence questions, the blank is shown as ____.

## Meanings
1. Break the word into parts: *bene*-*volent* = "wishing well".
2. Think of a related word you know: *fragile* ↔ fragment; *ancient* ↔ antique.
3. Decide its tone (positive, negative, neutral) and drop options that don't match.
4. Put each remaining option in place of the word in a sentence and keep the one that reads naturally.

Watch out: hard words often have a near-miss distractor; choose the most precise meaning, not just a related one.

## Synonyms and antonyms
1. Check the question: **synonym** = same meaning, **antonym** = opposite.
2. Define the given word in your own words first.
3. For a synonym, find the option that could swap in without changing the sentence.
4. For an antonym, flip your definition, then look for that.

Example: *frugal* = careful with money, so its opposite is "spends freely".

Watch out: an antonym question will often include a synonym as a trap.

## Commonly confused words
1. Expand contractions: *they're* = they are, *it's* = it is. If the expansion fits, use the apostrophe form.
2. **Affect** is usually the verb (to act on); **effect** is usually the noun (the result). "A" for action, "E" for end result.
3. **Than** compares; **then** is about time. **Too** means excess or "also"; **to** is direction.
4. For look-alike pairs, use a spelling hook (see the list below).

- Compl**e**ment completes; compl**i**ment is praise ("I like it")
- Princip**al** is a person, your "pal"; princip**le** is a rule
- Station**a**ry stays still (a for "at rest"); station**e**ry is paper (e for "envelope")
- Discr**ee**t keeps secrets; discr**e**te means separate

Watch out: *its* (belonging to it) never has an apostrophe.

## Words in context
1. Read the whole sentence and find the clue: a reason ("because…"), a result ("so…") or a contrast ("but…").
2. Predict your own word for the blank before you look at the options.
3. Choose the option closest to your prediction.
4. Reread the sentence with it in place to check grammar and meaning.

Example: "The bridge was ____, so engineers closed it": the clue "closed" points to something like *falling apart*.

Watch out: an option can be a real word with the right topic but the wrong tone.
