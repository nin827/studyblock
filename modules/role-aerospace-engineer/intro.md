# Applied scenarios (Aerospace Engineer)

Scenario questions for the **Aerospace Engineer** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up vehicle with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: weights and forces, formulas with squares and square roots, natural logarithms, and sums of masses times distances. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Every rule (g = 9.81 N/kg, air density 1.2 kg/m³, the lift and rocket equations, how a centre of gravity is found, any pass mark) is stated in the question, and each part says how to round. These are textbook exercises, not real aircraft or rocket design.

## Thrust-to-weight and lift-off

A vehicle has a mass and an engine thrust. You find its weight and thrust-to-weight ratio, then decide whether it lifts off. Later you add up dry mass, propellant and payload against a stated launch rule, and at Expert+ you follow the acceleration as propellant burns away and the vehicle gets lighter.

## Lift and stall speed

An aircraft flies at a speed with a stated wing area and lift coefficient. You find the dynamic pressure and the lift, and whether the lift carries the weight. Later you work backwards to the wing area needed and pick from a list of wings, and at Expert+ you find the stall speed, the approach speed and whether it is under an airfield limit in km/h.

## Rocket equation and staging

A booster burns from a starting mass to a final mass at a given exhaust speed. You find the mass ratio and the change in speed, and whether it meets a mission target. Later you work back from a target to the propellant needed and whether a tank is big enough, and at Expert+ two stages are added together, with the first stage's empty structure dropped between them.

## Mass and balance

A light aircraft is loaded from a table of masses and arms. You find the total mass and the centre of gravity, and whether it is inside stated limits. Later you follow the centre of gravity as fuel is burned, and at Expert+ an aircraft is outside its limits and you find how much baggage must move between holds, and whether there is room.
