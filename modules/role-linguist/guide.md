## General

Every rule is stated in the prompt, so apply exactly those rules and nothing else.

1. Read the rules or the glossary first, then the task.
2. Do one piece at a time: one word, one affix, one count.
3. Check your answer against the stated rule, not against how it sounds.
4. Chained parts use the earlier result, but every value here is exact, so no rounding is needed.

## Editing agreement, tense and punctuation

Check each verb against its subject, each determiner against its noun, each pronoun against the person or thing it refers to, and each time word against the tense of its verb. Words between a subject and its verb do not change the subject. When the passage states a convention (for example, collective nouns are singular), follow it even if you would say it differently. Name the rule, then say which way the word changes.

## Readability index

ASL is words divided by sentences. ASW is syllables divided by words. Then apply the stated formula and compare with the stated cut-offs; a value equal to a cut-off is in the higher band. Splitting sentences changes only the sentence count, so ASL changes and ASW does not. To work backwards, say how small ASL must be, then divide the words by that limit and go up to the next whole sentence.

## Building and decoding words

Join the pieces in order (prefix, root, suffix), then apply the spelling rules: drop a final e before a vowel suffix, change a final y to i before -ness and -ly, and double the final consonant of the listed short verbs. The meaning is the root meaning with each affix meaning added. The last suffix decides the word class; with only a prefix the root's class stays. To decode a word, remove the affixes first and then undo any spelling change to find the root.

## Translating a made-up language

Translate word by word using the vocabulary, then arrange the words in the stated order and add the markers: a plural suffix on plural nouns (and on adjectives when agreement is stated), the past particle right after the verb, and the negation particle right before it. Going back to English, remove the markers first, then restore English order and use "the" before every noun. When a rule is not stated, find it by comparing an example with the plain vocabulary words.
