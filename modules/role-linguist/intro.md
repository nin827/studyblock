# Applied scenarios (Linguist)

Scenario questions for the **Linguist** role, from Medium to Expert+. The Editor side corrects text and judges how readable it is. The Translator side builds and decodes words and translates with stated rules. Every rule a question depends on is written in its prompt, so you reason from the facts given, not from memory of exam rules.

Each question has two to four parts, and each part uses a different skill: spot the faulty word, type the corrected form, name the rule, score a passage, build a word, work out its meaning and word class, or translate. At Expert and Expert+ some parts are segmented choices (Rule | Fix, Prefix | Suffix). The calculator is on, and the counts from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

English is British. The readability index and the made-up language are inventions for practice: they are not real formulas or real languages, and the numbers are exact, so no rounding is needed.

## Editing agreement, tense and punctuation

A sentence (or, at Expert+, a three-sentence passage) with numbered words and exactly one wrong word. Find it, correct it and name the rule: subject–verb agreement, tense, determiners such as this/these, pronouns such as himself/themselves, or apostrophes.

## Readability index

A made-up Plain Index, ASL + 10 × ASW, worked from word, sentence and syllable counts and sorted into stated bands. Higher levels split sentences, compare two passages and work backwards to the number of sentences a passage needs.

## Building and decoding words

Join a prefix, a root and a suffix from a stated glossary, apply the stated spelling rules, then give the meaning and word class. At Expert+ you start from the finished word and take it apart.

## Translating a made-up language

A small invented language with a stated word order, plural suffix, past particle and (later) negation and adjective agreement. Translate both ways, count the marked words and, at Expert+, work out the unstated suffix and particle from two examples.
