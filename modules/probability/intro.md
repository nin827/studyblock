# Probability

How likely is something to happen? Probability puts a number on it, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Here you work it out by counting outcomes: dice, coins, spinners, playing cards, letter tiles and bags of marbles.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Basic probability | One event: favourable outcomes ÷ all outcomes |
| Combined events | Two or more trials: "and", "or", "at least one", with and without replacement |
| Counting | Arrangements, codes, podium places and teams (permutations and combinations) |
| Expected value | The average win, score or profit per game over the long run |

**Answer formats**

- **Probabilities** are fractions in simplest form, typed like `3/8`, with no spaces around the slash. `6/16` counts as wrong when the answer is `3/8`.
- **Counting** answers are whole numbers.
- **Expected values** are rounded to 2 decimal places (answers within 0.005 count). A loss is negative, so type the minus sign.

**Levels:** Easy, Medium and Hard, plus **Expert**: multi-step problems with several parts. Work backwards from a probability to what's in the bag, use tree diagrams and Venn diagrams for conditional probability ("given that…"), count with restrictions (letters kept together, committees with at least one of a group), and find the prize that makes a game fair or a missing value in a probability table. Probabilities in tables are fractions in simplest form like `7/20`, as on the other levels, and a missing probability is typed as a fraction too.

A standard deck has 52 cards: four suits of 13 (hearts and diamonds are red, clubs and spades are black), and the face cards are the jack, queen and king.
