# Ecology

Put numbers to living systems: how much energy moves up a food chain, how many animals live in a field you can't count one by one, how fast a population grows, and how diverse a habitat is.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `efficiency = energy passed on ÷ energy available × 100` | % transferred between trophic levels |
| `N = (M × C) ÷ R` | Lincoln–Petersen mark–recapture estimate |
| `total = mean per quadrat × field area ÷ quadrat area` | scaling up quadrat counts |
| `growth rate = (births − deaths) ÷ population × 100` | % change per year |
| `D = 1 − Σ n(n − 1) ÷ (N(N − 1))` | Simpson's diversity index (near 1 = diverse) |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | The 10% rule, simple mark–recapture, doubling, species richness and percentage cover |
| Medium | Efficiencies to 2 decimal places, quadrats, percentage growth, Simpson's index |
| Hard | Several transfers, 50 cm quadrats, per-1000 rates, comparing two sites, multi-part questions |

**Answer formats:** type percentages without the % sign; a falling population has a negative growth rate. Each question says how to round, and states its assumptions (such as a closed population for mark–recapture).
