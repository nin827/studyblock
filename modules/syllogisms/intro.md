# Syllogisms & Arguments

An argument is **valid** when its conclusion **must** be true whenever its premises are true. Whether the premises are true in real life doesn't matter, so the categorical questions use made-up words (zorbs, plinks, wugs) to keep real-world knowledge out of the way.

Every answer was checked by trying every possible case: every Venn diagram for the categorical questions, and every true/false combination for the if-then ones.

## Categorical syllogisms

Statements of four kinds: **All** X are Y, **No** X are Y, **Some** X are Y, and **Some** X are **not** Y. Assume every group mentioned has at least one member.

| Level | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Valid or invalid? (mostly "all" and "no") |
| Medium | Which conclusion follows? (or none) |
| Hard | Judge several conclusions, or one with "some … not" in play |
| Expert | Three premises chained through four groups: which conclusion follows? |

## If-then arguments

| Level | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | The four classic forms: valid or invalid? |
| Medium | What follows from a rule and a fact, and which statement means the same |
| Hard | Two rules worded with "only if" and "unless", plus a fact |
| Expert | Four statements with "either … or" and "not both": true, false or can't tell? |
