# Applied scenarios (Mechanical Engineer)

Scenario questions for the **Mechanical Engineer** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: tooth counts and ratios, torque and power, stress and factors of safety, limits and clearances, areas, pressures and flows, and choosing from a list of standard sizes. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Every rule (the torque formula, π = 3.14, the stress areas of the bolts, how shear yield relates to tensile yield, how a stack-up is combined, how pressure and flow relate) is stated in the question, and each part says how to round. Forces are in N or kN, stresses in N/mm² (the same as MPa), lengths in mm unless stated, and 1 N/mm² = 10 bar.

## Gear train and motor sizing

A two-stage gearbox is described by its tooth counts. You find the overall ratio and the output speed, then the output torque from a motor's power and speed and a stated efficiency. Later you work backwards from a load torque to pick the smallest motor from a list, and at Expert+ you allow for the efficiency of each stage and decide whether the drive meets a speed band and a torque.

## Bolt and shaft stress check

A load is shared by several bolts. You find the force on each, the stress from a stated stress area, and the factor of safety against yield. Later you work out the area a bolt needs and pick the smallest size from a list. At Expert+ you check a solid shaft in torsion from the power it carries and pick its smallest standard diameter.

## Fits and assembly tolerances

A hole and a shaft are given by their limits in millimetres. You find the largest and smallest clearance in micrometres and name the fit as clearance, transition or interference. Later four dimensions are stacked in a housing: you find the worst-case gaps and whether the assembly always works, and at Expert+ you compare the worst case with a root-sum-square stack.

## Hydraulic cylinder and pump

A cylinder has to push a load. You find the piston area and the pressure needed, then the speed the pump gives it. Later you find the hydraulic power and pick the smallest motor to drive the pump, and at Expert+ a double-acting cylinder has a smaller area on the retract stroke, which changes its force and its time.
