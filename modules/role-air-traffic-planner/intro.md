# Applied scenarios (Air Traffic Planner)

Scenario questions for the **Air Traffic Planner** role, from Medium to Expert+. Each one is a single made-up scenario with several linked answers, and each answer uses a different skill: rates and clock arithmetic, tables, rounding down or up, and a yes or no decision. A later part usually builds on an earlier one, so work in order. The calculator is on, and the numbers from the prompt are already in it where that helps.

Speeds are in knots (kt), distances in nautical miles (nm), fuel in kg. Times use the 24-hour clock in one fixed local time zone. Every part says how to round.

## Runway arrival spacing

A stated table gives the minimum separation for each leader and follower category. Two aircraft at one speed give a time gap; a stream of aircraft gives a total time and an hourly capacity. At Expert+ the aircraft fly at different speeds on the last leg, so the spacing at the entry gate must be larger than the spacing needed at the threshold.

## Departure slots and delays

Departure slots are 15-minute blocks with a stated capacity. Flights are taken in order and each gets the earliest slot with room. You count the delayed flights, then find total, longest and average delay and the last departure. At Expert+ the runway closes for a slot or two, and you weigh the extra delay against a cut-off time.

## In-trail separation on a route

A faster aircraft following a slower one closes the gap at the difference of their speeds. You find the time until the gap reaches the minimum, the speed the follower must not exceed, and whether it can hold its speed to a fix. At Expert+ separation is measured as a time gap at the fix.

## Holding and diversion fuel

A holding pattern takes whole laps, so the delay is rounded up to laps and the fuel follows from the lap time and burn rate. You check the fuel left against the approach and final reserve, and at Expert+ the fuel to divert to an alternate airport decides how many laps are affordable.
