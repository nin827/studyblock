# Kinematics: how to solve

## General

- Write down what you know (u, v, a, t, s or d) and what you want. Then pick the formula that links exactly those.
- Units are metres, seconds, m/s and m/s², so you can substitute the numbers straight in.
- Rearrange the formula before you substitute, so the unknown is on its own.
- Easy and medium answers are whole numbers. On hard questions keep full precision and round to 2 decimal places only at the end.

## Speed

1. Use `v = d / t`.
2. Rearrange it for what you want: `d = v × t` or `t = d / v`.
3. Substitute and work it out.

Example: 120 m in 8 s gives `v = 120 / 8 = 15` m/s.

**Watch out:** check that it makes sense. Distance divided by time gives a speed, and a speed times a time gives a distance. Don't multiply when you should divide.

**Expert:** average speed is total distance ÷ total time, never the mean of the speeds. For catch-up and meeting problems, write each mover's distance as speed × its own time (one starts later) and set the distances equal, or for a head-on meeting, make them add up to the gap.

## Acceleration

1. Use `a = (v - u) / t`, or `v = u + at` in its other forms.
2. Rearrange it: `v = u + at`, `u = v - at`, or `t = (v - u) / a`.
3. Substitute, putting negative numbers in brackets, and work out the brackets first.

Example: from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 3 s gives `a = (10 - 4) / 3 = 2` m/s².

**Watch out:** when something slows down, `v - u` is negative, and so is the acceleration.

**Expert:** sketch the velocity-time graph. Its gradient is the acceleration and the area under it is the distance. From two readings, the gradient gives a, then work back to t = 0 with `u = v - at`. Convert km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6.

## Equations of motion

1. List u, v, a, t and s, and mark the one you want. One of the five will be missing from the question.
2. If t is missing, use `v² = u² + 2as`. If v is missing, use `s = ut + ½at²`.
3. Rearrange for the unknown. For example `a = 2(s - ut) / t²` or `s = (v² - u²) / 2a`.
4. Substitute. "From rest" means u = 0.

Example: from rest at 2 m/s² for 5 s gives `s = 0 × 5 + ½ × 2 × 5² = 25` m.

**Watch out:** don't drop the ½ in `½at²`, and remember to take the square root when you find v from v².

**Expert:** stopping distance = thinking distance (steady speed × reaction time) + braking distance (`u² / 2a`). Working backwards from a gap gives a quadratic in u: keep the positive root. For a throw from a cliff, take up as positive, so the ground is at s = -H, and keep the positive root of the time quadratic. For a chase from rest, set `½at²` equal to the other car's steady-speed distance.
