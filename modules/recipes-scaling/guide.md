# Recipes and Scaling: how to solve

## General

- Use the conversion factor in the question; kitchen measures vary from country to country.
- Scale first, convert second (or the other way round), but round only once, at the end.
- Whole things (eggs, bags, trays, bakes) are counted, not measured: when you need part of one, round **up**.

## Scaling recipes

1. Find the scale factor: new servings ÷ recipe servings. 4 → 6 people is × 1.5.
2. Multiply every amount by the factor. Or find one serving first (amount ÷ servings), then multiply by the new number.
3. For a ratio a : b, add the parts (a + b), divide the total by that, then multiply by a.
4. The most servings from what you have: for each ingredient, have ÷ (amount per serving), rounded down. The smallest one wins.

Example: a recipe for 4 uses 300 g flour. For 6: `300 × 6 / 4 = 450` g.

Baker's percentages: flour is 100% and everything else is a percent of the flour. The dough is (100 + all the other percents)% of the flour, so divide the dough mass by that, written as a decimal.

**Watch out:** the most servings is limited by the ingredient that runs out first, not the one you have most of.

## Kitchen measures

1. Smaller unit, bigger number: multiply. Bigger unit, smaller number: divide.
2. Turn fractions of a cup into decimals first: ¼ = 0.25, ½ = 0.5, ¾ = 0.75; ⅓ cup is exactly 16 tsp.
3. Grams to cups: divide by the grams in one cup of that ingredient. Cups to grams: multiply.
4. Pounds and ounces: turn everything into ounces first (1 lb = 16 oz), then into grams.
5. °C to °F: × 9 / 5, then + 32. °F to °C: − 32 first, then × 5 / 9.

Example: 2½ cups × 16 = 40 tbsp.

**Watch out:** for °F to °C, subtract 32 before multiplying, not after.

## Cooking times

1. Roasting rules like "20 min per 500 g, plus 20 min": count the 500 g lots, multiply, then add the extra.
2. Trays: items ÷ items per bake, rounded up, gives the number of bakes. Between bakes there is one fewer swap than bakes.
3. Start times: work backwards from serving time. Take off the rest first (the food is out of the oven then), then the cooking time.
4. A side dish that comes out at serving time goes in that many minutes before serving.

Example: a 2 kg chicken at 20 min per 500 g + 20 min is `4 × 20 + 20 = 100` min.

**Watch out:** resting time is after the oven, so the roast has to come out before dinner.
