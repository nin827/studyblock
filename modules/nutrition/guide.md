# Nutrition: how to solve

## General

- Note the facts the question gives: kcal per gram, the kJ factor, the portion size or the reference intake.
- Work out each step with full precision and round only at the end.
- Label values are per 100 g, so scale them to the portion before comparing.
- These are arithmetic exercises, not advice about what to eat.

## Energy from food

1. Multiply grams of carbohydrate or protein by 4, and grams of fat by 9.
2. For a total, add the energy from each nutrient.
3. To find grams from kcal, divide by the kcal per gram.
4. If you're given a percentage of the total energy instead, find that share first (total × % ÷ 100), then divide by the kcal per gram.

Example: 20 g of fat gives 20 × 9 = 180 kcal. If 30% of a 600 kcal meal is fat, that's 180 kcal, so 180 ÷ 9 = 20 g of fat.

**Watch out:** fat is 9 kcal per gram, not 4.

## Energy units

1. kcal → kJ: multiply by 4.184.
2. kJ → kcal: divide by 4.184.
3. For MJ, change between kJ and MJ with × or ÷ 1000.

Example: 500 kcal × 4.184 = 2092 kJ.

**Watch out:** the kJ number is always the bigger one.

## Food labels

1. Per portion = per 100 g × portion size ÷ 100.
2. % of RI = amount ÷ reference intake × 100.
3. To find how many grams contain an amount, use amount × 100 ÷ amount per 100 g.

Example: 12 g of sugars per 100 g in a 40 g portion is 12 × 40 ÷ 100 = 4.8 g.

**Watch out:** use the portion amount, not the per-100 g amount, when you work out a % of the RI for one portion.

## Energy balance

1. Energy balance = energy in − energy out. It is negative when more is used than taken in.
2. For a week with the same daily figures, multiply the daily balance by 7.
3. A mean is the total ÷ the number of days.
4. % of energy from a nutrient = its kcal ÷ total kcal × 100.

Example: 2200 kcal in and 2450 kcal out gives 2200 − 2450 = −250 kcal.

**Watch out:** turn grams into kcal before working out a percentage of energy.
