# Thermal Physics

Practise how energy changes the temperature and state of things: heating water in a kettle, melting ice, converting between temperature scales and working out what happens when hot and cold things mix. Every constant you need (specific heat capacity, latent heat, the kelvin offset) is given in the question, so nothing needs memorising.

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `E = m × c × ΔT` | energy to change temperature (c in J/kg °C) |
| `E = m × L` | energy to melt or boil, at constant temperature (L in J/kg or kJ/kg) |
| `K = °C + 273` | kelvin (hard questions say + 273.15) |
| `°F = °C × 9/5 + 32` | Fahrenheit |
| `E = P × t` | energy from a heater: power (W) × time (s) |
| `efficiency = useful / total × 100` | as a percentage |
| energy lost = energy gained | when hot and cold things mix (no heat lost) |

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Small whole numbers, matching units, whole-number answers |
| Medium | Values in g, kJ, kJ/kg, kW or minutes to convert, still whole-number answers |
| Hard | Decimals, two-stage heating curves, mixing different materials, efficiency; answers rounded to 2 decimal places, some in several parts |
| Expert | Multi-step problems you set up yourself: correcting an experiment for heat lost, full heating curves with timings, made-up and uncalibrated temperature scales, ice that may not all melt, three-body mixing worked backwards. Each part says how to round |

> [!TIP]
> Work in kg, J and s. On hard questions keep full precision and round to 2 decimal places at the end (answers within 0.005 are accepted). Percentages are typed without the `%` sign. The calculator starts with the question's numbers; a label like `_m_g` or `_E_kJ` shows the unit of that value.
