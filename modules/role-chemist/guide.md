## General

Chemistry scenarios run on moles. Convert a mass to moles (mass ÷ molar mass), carry the moles through the balanced equation, and convert back to whatever is asked.

1. Read the stated constants: relative atomic masses, R, and the molar volume at RTP.
2. Work in order. Each part usually feeds the next.
3. Round only as each part asks. A rounded earlier answer gives the same later answer.
4. For a Yes/No part, find the number first, then compare it with the stated rule.

## Yield and purity

Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100. The theoretical mass comes from the mole ratio in the equation.

For an empirical formula, divide each mass by its relative atomic mass, then divide by the smallest to get the ratio.

To find purity, work backwards: product moles, then moles of pure reactant, then its mass. Purity = pure mass ÷ sample mass × 100.

## Titration and strength

Millimoles = concentration (mol/dm³) × volume (cm³). Use the equation ratio to move from the base to the acid. Concentration = millimoles ÷ cm³.

For dilution, the moles stay the same: c₁V₁ = c₂V₂.

For an impure solid made up to 250 cm³, a 25.0 cm³ portion holds one tenth of the NaOH.

## Gas and vessel safety

Moles of gas come from the moles of reactant and the equation. At RTP, volume = moles × 24 dm³.

In a rigid vessel, P = nRT ÷ V (kPa, dm³, kelvin). Pressure rises in proportion to the kelvin temperature when heated.

Working backwards from a rating, n = PV ÷ RT gives the most gas, and the equation gives the most reactant.

## Scale-up and batch cost

The yield tells you how much to make in theory: theoretical product = target ÷ yield. Use molar masses to turn product into reactant.

Batch cost = reactant mass × price + fixed cost. Cost per kg = batch cost ÷ kg delivered. Compare plans by cost per kg of product.
