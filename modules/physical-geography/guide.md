# Physical Geography: how to solve

## General
- Picture the map: name the continent first, then the feature on it.
- "Longest", "highest", "largest" questions are about one continent at a time, so learn one record per continent.
- Typed answers ignore capitals, but spelling counts. Follow the question on whether to include words like "River" or "Mount".

## Rivers
1. Longest per continent: Africa **Nile**, South America **Amazon**, Asia **Yangtze**, Europe **Volga**, North America Missouri–Mississippi.
2. Rivers through capitals: Thames (London), Seine (Paris), Danube (Vienna, Budapest and others), Nile (Cairo), Tigris (Baghdad).
3. Follow the river to its end: most reach an ocean, but the Volga ends in the Caspian Sea.
4. Border rivers: the Rio Grande runs between the USA and Mexico.

Watch out: the Euphrates runs near Baghdad, but the city sits on the Tigris.

## Mountains
1. Highest per continent: Asia Everest, South America Aconcagua, North America Denali, Africa Kilimanjaro, Europe Elbrus, Antarctica Vinson.
2. Ranges by place: Himalayas (Asia), Andes (west side of South America), Alps (central Europe, highest Mont Blanc), Pyrenees (France–Spain), Atlas (northwest Africa), Urals (Europe–Asia boundary).
3. Everest sits on the Nepal–China (Tibet) border; K2 is second-highest, on the Pakistan–China border.

Watch out: Mont Blanc is the Alps' highest peak, but Elbrus in the Caucasus is often counted as Europe's highest.

## Deserts and lakes
1. A desert is defined by very little **precipitation**, not heat: Antarctica counts as a cold desert.
2. Place each desert: Sahara (North Africa, largest hot desert), Gobi (China and Mongolia), Atacama (Chile, one of the driest), Kalahari (Botswana and its neighbours).
3. Lake records: deepest is Baikal (Russia); Africa's largest is Victoria (shared by Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania); Titicaca sits high on the Peru–Bolivia border.
4. Lowest land: the shore of the Dead Sea.

Watch out: the Caspian Sea is the largest lake by area, but Baikal is the deepest.

## Climate and biomes
1. Latitude runs from 0° at the Equator to 90° at each pole.
2. The tropics lie between the Tropic of **C**ancer (north, overhead in June) and the Tropic of **C**apricorn (south, overhead in December).
3. Match the biome to its temperature and rain (see the list below).
4. Named patterns: a monsoon is a seasonal wind shift bringing heavy rain; a Mediterranean climate has hot dry summers and mild wet winters.

- Hot and wet all year → tropical rainforest
- Hot with a long dry season, grass and scattered trees → savanna
- Cold northern conifer forest → taiga
- Frozen subsoil, no trees → tundra

Watch out: taiga (forest) and tundra (treeless) are both cold; tundra is further north.
