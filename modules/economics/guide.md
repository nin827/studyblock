# Economics: how to solve

## General

- Write down what the question gives you, and use the formula it states.
- Prices are in dollars. Type answers without `$` or `%`.
- Keep full precision in the calculator and round only at the end.

## Supply and demand

1. Set Qd = Qs.
2. Move the P terms to one side and the numbers to the other, then divide to get P.
3. Put P back into either equation to get Q.
4. At a fixed price, find Qd and Qs. Qd > Qs is a shortage, and Qs > Qd is a surplus.

Example: 200 − 4P = 20 + 2P gives 180 = 6P, so P = 30 and Q = 80.

**Watch out:** a shift "by 50 units at every price" changes the number at the front of the demand equation, not the slope.

**Expert.** *Unknown supply intercept:* at the equilibrium price Qs = Qd, so c = Qd − d × P. *Surplus:* consumer surplus = ½ × Q × (a ÷ b − P) and producer surplus = ½ × Q × (P − (−c ÷ d)), the triangles up to where each line meets the price axis. *Both curves shift:* change both front numbers, then solve as usual.

## Elasticity

1. % change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. With the midpoint method, divide by the average of old and new instead.
2. PED = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price. Use its size and ignore the minus sign.
3. Above 1 is elastic, below 1 inelastic, exactly 1 unit elastic, and 0 perfectly inelastic.
4. Elastic: revenue moves the opposite way to the price. Inelastic: revenue moves the same way.

Example: price +10% and quantity −25% gives PED = 25 ÷ 10 = 2.5, which is elastic.

**Watch out:** the midpoint and "from the original" methods give different answers. Use the one the question names.

**Expert.** *Point elasticity* on `Qd = a − bP`: PED = b × P ÷ Q; revenue is highest at P = a ÷ (2b), where PED = 1. *Sales target:* % change in price = % change in quantity ÷ PED, in the opposite direction. *Cross and income elasticity* keep their sign: cross > 0 substitutes, < 0 complements; income < 0 inferior, 0 to 1 necessity, > 1 luxury.

## Macro measures

1. Growth or inflation % = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
2. GDP per person = GDP ÷ population. Billions ÷ millions leaves a factor of 1000.
3. Real GDP = nominal GDP × 100 ÷ deflator. Then compare real values for real growth.
4. A price index = cost this year ÷ cost in the base year × 100.

Example: GDP $400 billion with 20 million people is 400 ÷ 20 × 1000 = $20,000 per person.

**Watch out:** don't subtract the inflation rate from nominal growth. Work out each year's real GDP first.

**Expert.** *Exact real rate* = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. *Average yearly inflation* over k years = (CPI ratio)^(1/k) − 1, not the total ÷ k. *Expenditure GDP:* GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), so G = GDP − C − I − (X − M). *Per-person growth* = (1 + GDP growth) ÷ (1 + population growth) − 1; years to double = ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + rate), rounded up.

## Taxes and price controls

1. A ceiling below equilibrium causes a shortage, and a floor above it a surplus. Only the smaller of Qd and Qs actually trades.
2. A tax of t on sellers turns supply into Qs = c + d(P − t). Solve with demand to get the price buyers pay.
3. Sellers keep the buyers' price − t. Tax revenue = t × the new quantity.
4. Deadweight loss = ½ × t × (Q before − Q after). The buyers' share = rise in the buyers' price ÷ t.

Example: with Qd = 200 − 4P and Qs = 20 + 2P, a $3 tax gives 200 − 4P = 20 + 2(P − 3), so P = 31 and Q = 76.

**Watch out:** with ordinary sloped curves the tax is shared, so the buyers' price rises by less than the tax.

**Expert.** *Tax for a revenue target:* the quantity with a tax t is Q − (b × d ÷ (b + d)) × t; set t × that quantity equal to the target and solve the quadratic (the smaller root). *Subsidy s:* supply becomes c + d(P + s); sellers get P + s and the cost is s × Q. *Ceiling:* the quantity supplied at the ceiling trades; the black-market price is (a − Q) ÷ b, and the deadweight loss is ½ × (Q before − Q traded) × (black-market price − ceiling).
