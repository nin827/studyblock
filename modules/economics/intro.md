# Economics

The textbook models behind prices and the wider economy, worked out with numbers. Markets use straight-line demand and supply, written like `Qd = 200 − 4P` and `Qs = 20 + 2P`, with P the price in dollars. The countries are made up, so the numbers are practice, not real statistics.

| Subtopic | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Supply and demand | Find the equilibrium price and quantity, shortages and surpluses at a fixed price, and what happens when demand shifts |
| Elasticity | Price elasticity of demand (PED), elastic or inelastic, and what a price change does to revenue |
| Macro measures | GDP growth, GDP per person, inflation from a price index, and real versus nominal GDP |
| Taxes and price controls | Price ceilings and floors, who pays a tax, tax revenue and deadweight loss |

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Qd = Qs` | equilibrium |
| `PED = % change in Qd ÷ % change in P` | given as its size, without the minus sign |
| `growth % = (new − old) ÷ old × 100` | GDP growth or inflation |
| `real GDP = nominal GDP × 100 ÷ deflator` | removes price changes |

**Answer formats**

- **Money and quantities:** type the number only, no `$`. Each question says whether to round to 2 decimal places (answers within 0.005 count).
- **Percentages:** type the number without the `%` sign, with a minus for a fall.
- Each question says which method to use, for example the midpoint method for elasticity.

**Levels.** Easy and Medium use one formula at a time; Hard adds multi-step and multi-part questions. **Expert** questions need you to set up the problem and combine ideas: find a supply curve from its equilibrium then shift demand, consumer and producer surplus, both curves shifting at once, point elasticity and the revenue-maximising price, the price change needed for a sales target, cross and income elasticity, exact real interest rates, average yearly inflation, GDP by expenditure, years for GDP per person to double, the tax that raises a target revenue, subsidies, and the black-market price and deadweight loss under a price ceiling.
