# Body Measurements: how to solve

## General

- Read the formula and the conversion factors in the question, and write down what you are given.
- Put every measurement into the units the formula needs (kg and m for BMI, the same units for a ratio) before you calculate.
- Keep full precision while you work and round only at the end, to what the question asks.
- These are calculations only: none of them is a judgement about anyone's health.

## Unit conversions

1. Decide whether the answer should be bigger or smaller: pounds outnumber kilograms, and centimetres outnumber inches.
2. Multiply when going to the smaller unit (kg → lb, in → cm) and divide when going to the bigger one.
3. For feet and inches, change everything to inches first (feet × 12 + inches).
4. For temperature, use °F = °C × 9/5 + 32, or °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9.

Example: 70 kg × 2.2046 = 154.322, so about 154.3 lb.

**Watch out:** in °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9, subtract the 32 before you multiply.

## BMI

1. Change the height to metres (cm ÷ 100) and the mass to kg if needed.
2. Square the height.
3. Divide the mass by the squared height. To find a mass instead, use mass = BMI × height².
4. For a range question, compare the BMI with the boundaries 18.5, 25 and 30.

Example: 68 kg and 1.75 m gives 68 ÷ 1.75² = 68 ÷ 3.0625 ≈ 22.2.

**Watch out:** square the height, don't double it, and don't forget to change cm to m.

## Body ratios

1. For a ratio, put both measurements in the same units, then divide (waist ÷ height, or waist ÷ hip).
2. For a percentage change, find new − old, divide by the old value, then multiply by 100.
3. For a reverse percentage, divide the new value by the multiplier (1 + percentage ÷ 100).

Example: from 80 kg to 76 kg, (76 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = −5%.

**Watch out:** always divide by the old value. Taking the same percentage of the new value doesn't undo a percentage change.
