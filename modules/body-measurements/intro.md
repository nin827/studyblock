# Body Measurements

> [!WARNING] For learning only
> This isn't medical advice; for anything about your own health, ask a qualified professional.

Practise the arithmetic behind everyday body measurements: switching between metric and imperial units, working out BMI, and simple ratios and percentage changes. Every question gives you the formula and the conversion factors it needs.

| Conversion | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| kilograms → pounds | 1 kg = 2.2046 lb |
| stones → pounds | 1 st = 14 lb |
| inches → centimetres | 1 in = 2.54 cm |
| feet → inches | 1 ft = 12 in |
| °C → °F | °F = °C × 9/5 + 32 |

| Formula | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `BMI = mass (kg) ÷ height (m)²` | body mass index |
| `waist ÷ height` | waist-to-height ratio (same units) |
| `waist ÷ hip` | waist-to-hip ratio |
| `(new − old) ÷ old × 100` | percentage change |

BMI is a rough, population-level measure: it doesn't tell you about any one person's health. Some questions use the general adult categories (under 18.5, 18.5 to 24.9, 25 to 29.9, 30 or more), but only as a classification exercise.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | One conversion or one formula with whole-number inputs |
| Medium | Heights in cm, rearranged formulas, BMI ranges, percentage change |
| Hard | Imperial units, reverse percentages, and some two-part questions |

> [!TIP]
> Every question says how to round; answers within half of the last decimal place count (±0.05 for 1 decimal place). For percentages, type the number without the % sign, with a minus for a decrease.
