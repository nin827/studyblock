# Intervals & Scales

Music theory on paper: no audio, just note names. You'll count semitones on a piano keyboard, name intervals, build major and minor scales, and read key signatures.

## Notes and spelling

- The musical alphabet is **A B C D E F G**, then it starts again.
- A **semitone** is the very next key on a keyboard, black or white. **E–F** and **B–C** are a semitone apart; the other neighbouring letters are a tone (two semitones) apart.
- **♯ (sharp)** raises a note by a semitone, **♭ (flat)** lowers it by one.
- The same key can have two names, like F♯ and G♭. They are **enharmonic**. Which name is right depends on the letter: a major 3rd above D is **F♯**, because D to F spans three letter names. G♭ would be a diminished 4th.

## Answer formats

- Counts (semitones, sharps or flats) are typed as whole numbers.
- Notes, interval names and keys are **multiple choice**, and whole scales are built from **note blocks**, so spelling never depends on how you type ♯ or ♭.
- Only one kind of question needs a double sharp (𝄪): the raised 7th of G♯ and D♯ harmonic minor, which is F𝄪 and C𝄪 (Hard and Expert). It raises a note that is already sharp in the key signature.

| Level | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | White keys, simple intervals, keys with up to 2–3 sharps or flats |
| Medium | Sharps and flats, all major, minor and perfect intervals, natural minor scales |
| Hard | Augmented and diminished intervals, harmonic minor, keys up to 7 sharps or flats, two-part questions |
| Expert | Multi-step: working backwards, unusual spellings like E♯ and C♭, inversions, compound intervals, parallel and enharmonic keys |

**Expert** asks you to combine ideas: find the note *below* a given one, spell a key with a set letter (E♯, B♯, C♭, F♭), turn an interval upside down, name intervals bigger than an octave (9th to 13th), work out which scale a note belongs to, and read a key from its signature plus a raised 7th.

There's no calculator here: counting on the keyboard is the point.
